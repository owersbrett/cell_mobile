# Organelle Rush — How to Play

> Player-facing. Anyone reading this should understand the game.
> This is one entry in the Manual and a source file for the deployable Manual app.
> **Status: UNBUILT — this entry is a forward spec. Rules are locked; game is not yet coded.**

**Scale:** Organelle · **Type:** Solo (chase the high score) · **Length:** ~3 minutes total (8 mini-rounds)

---

## The goal

Eight tiny games fire back-to-back, each lasting about 20 seconds. Every mini-round is about a
different part of the cell. Rack up as many points as you can across all eight rounds — the clock
never stops between them.

---

## The eight rounds

**Round 1 — The Zoom**
A cross-section of a cell appears. Glowing rings pulse at each layer: Cell Wall, Plasma Membrane,
Cytoplasm, Nucleus, Nuclear Membrane, Nucleolus, Ribosome. Tap each ring before it fades. Hit all
seven for a perfect-run bonus.

**Round 2 — ER Lanes**
Two factory lanes run across the screen. The bumpy lane (Rough ER) produces protein blobs. The
smooth lane (Smooth ER) produces lipid bubbles. Tap the correct product in each lane — wrong lane
taps cost you points. Speed ramps up as the round goes on.

**Round 3 — Golgi Sort**
Vesicles drift in from the left labeled with their cargo: protein, lipid, or waste. Three exit
chutes are on the right: Export, Membrane, Recycle. Swipe each vesicle into the correct chute
before it auto-routes incorrectly. Fast, satisfying, punishes hesitation.

**Round 4 — DNA Unzip**
A DNA double helix splits open. Nucleotide bases fall from above. Tap the correct matching base
to bond it to the exposed template strand: A pairs with T, G pairs with C. Every time you bond
a DNA base correctly, an RNA copy spins off to the side.

**Round 5 — Ribosome Line**
An mRNA strand scrolls through a ribosome. A codon (three-letter sequence) lights up. Tap the
correct amino acid from the pool. A short codon table at the top tells you which codon maps to
which amino acid. Each correct tap grows the protein chain at the bottom.

**Round 6 — Mitochondria Burn**
Glucose hexagons float in from the left; oxygen pairs float in from the right. Tap a glucose,
then tap a nearby oxygen within 1.5 seconds to combust them into an ATP coin. Collect the coin.
Leave glucose piling up without oxygen and a fermentation penalty timer starts.

**Round 7 — Spindle Fire**
A cell is about to divide. Chromosomes drift inside. Drag either centriole (top or bottom of
the cell) to aim a spindle fiber, then release to fire. You have 3 shots per centriole. Each
chromosome you connect earns points; any chromosome left unattached when the division fires
costs you.

**Round 8 — Peroxisome Sweep**
Free radicals — jagged, erratic sparks — are swarming the cytoplasm. Drag your peroxisomes
(the round glowing orbs) into their path to neutralize them. Hitting multiple radicals in a
single peroxisome pass chains into a combo multiplier. The spawn rate speeds up — keep sweeping.

---

## How you score

Each round has its own scoring (taps correct, combos, time bonuses). At the end of all eight
rounds the totals are summed. A solid run lands around 400–700 points; a perfect run can clear 900.

There is no fail condition — even if you miss every tap you finish all eight rounds.

---

## After each round

A brief reveal card appears: one fact about the organelle you just played, and where it shows
up in a potato. It fades in and out automatically — you can tap to dismiss it early if you want
to start the next round faster.

---

## Tips

- Round 1 (The Zoom) is free points — the targets are large and slow. Use it to get oriented.
- Round 4 (DNA) is the toughest cognitively. The pairing rules are on screen — glance up if you
  forget which base pairs with which.
- Round 5 (Ribosomes): the codon table at the top is there the whole round. You don't have to
  memorize anything.
- In Round 3 (Golgi), hesitation is your enemy — the vesicle will auto-route wrong if you wait
  too long. Swipe first, second-guess never.
- Round 8 (Peroxisomes) gets chaotic fast. Prioritize chaining — a 3-in-a-row combo doubles
  your points for those hits.

---

## What it teaches

What a cell actually contains — and what every piece does. The eight rounds tour the cell from
its outermost wall to its smallest workers. By the end you've handled the DNA, assembled a protein,
burned glucose into ATP, and swept free radicals. That's not a metaphor — it's the real sequence
of events happening in every cell of every potato right now.
