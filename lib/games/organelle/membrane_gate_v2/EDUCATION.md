# EDUCATION.md — Membrane Gate v2

The educational component (E in GAMES). The lesson is identical to v1; the UX
pass only made the teaching *more legible*, never thinner.

## Learning objective

Understand **selective permeability**: a cell membrane is not a simple sieve. It
**actively selects** what crosses, by route. Players learn which substances the
cell imports and **which transport mechanism** each one uses.

## The science in the mechanic

The membrane is **default-impermeable** — keeping intruders out is free
(they bounce). **Importing is the work** (the player's taps). That framing is the
core idea: transport is selective and active, not passive leakage.

### Transport routes taught (each import lights its real structure)

| Substance | Route | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| O₂, CO₂ | **Simple diffusion** | Small nonpolar molecules dissolve straight through the lipid bilayer — no protein needed |
| H₂O | **Osmosis via aquaporins** | Water is polar; it crosses through dedicated aquaporin channels |
| Na⁺, K⁺ | **Facilitated — ion channels** | Charged ions cannot cross lipid; they pass through protein channels |
| Glucose | **Facilitated — glucose transporter** | Large/polar; carried by a specific transporter (GLUT) |
| Amino acids | **Facilitated — carrier protein** | Large/polar; carrier-mediated transport |

On every successful import the matching membrane protein **glows** and a
one-line route label pops (`Aquaporin`, `Ion channel`, …) — connecting the
action to the structure. Gases light **no** channel (they diffuse through the
lipid), reinforcing the diffusion-vs-facilitated distinction.

### What the membrane keeps out

Toxins, viruses, bacteria, heavy metals, and metabolic waste **bounce**. This
teaches the protective, barrier role of selective permeability — the membrane
defends the cell's internal environment (homeostasis).

## Why the v2 legibility changes are pedagogically better

The point of the game is **discrimination by transport rule**, not by reading 8px
labels. v1 bottlenecked the lesson on text under fast motion (and even put a
toxin and an ion in the same green). v2 makes "does the cell want this?" an
instant perceptual judgment (bright+ring = import via its route; dull+✕ = block),
so a learner's attention goes to **the biology of the choice** — which route,
which protein — instead of squinting. The route still has to be learned; the
go/no-go no longer fights perception.

## Misconceptions corrected

- *"The membrane is just a filter by size."* → No — charged/polar molecules need
  specific proteins even when small (ions); gases pass freely though they aren't
  the smallest. Route depends on chemistry, not size alone.
- *"Everything needs energy/a pump to enter."* → Simple diffusion and facilitated
  diffusion are passive (down the gradient); the game's "work" is the *selection*,
  modeling the cell's gatekeeping, not ATP cost.

## Potato tie-in

A potato-tuber cell imports water, ions, and glucose to build starch stores while
blocking rot microbes and toxins. Good gatekeeping = a firm tuber; a compromised
membrane = soft rot. Same lesson, edible stakes.
