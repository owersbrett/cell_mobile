# EDUCATION.md — Organelle Match

> The educational contract. The learning is IN the mechanic: every tap forces the player to
> map an organelle to the one job it does. Over a 60 s round the player sees each of the nine
> organelles multiple times, with rotating clue phrasings and a fact card on every answer.

---

## Learning objective

By the end of a round, a player can match each major cell organelle to its primary function:

| Organelle | Primary function (the takeaway) |
|---|---|
| **Nucleus** | Control center — stores the cell's DNA and directs its activity. |
| **Mitochondria** | Powerhouse — releases energy (ATP) by burning sugar with oxygen (cellular respiration). |
| **Ribosome** | Protein factory — reads RNA and assembles amino acids into proteins. |
| **Chloroplast** | Photosynthesis — captures sunlight to turn water + CO₂ into sugar (plants). |
| **Golgi apparatus** | Packaging & shipping — folds, tags, and sends proteins to their destinations. |
| **Lysosome** | Recycling — digests waste and breaks down worn-out cell parts with enzymes. |
| **Endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** | Synthesis & transport — builds lipids, ships proteins from ribosomes. |
| **Vacuole** | Storage — holds water, food, and waste; keeps plant cells firm. |
| **Cell membrane** | Boundary & gatekeeper — controls what enters and leaves the cell. |

---

## How the mechanic teaches

1. **Forced recall, not recognition.** The clue gives the FUNCTION and asks for the
   organelle — the harder, more durable direction of the mapping.
2. **Rotating phrasings.** Each organelle has three clue variants, so the player learns the
   concept, not a memorized sentence (e.g. mitochondria: "burns sugar with oxygen to make
   energy" / "powers the cell by making ATP" / "carries out cellular respiration").
3. **Fact card on every answer.** Correct or wrong, the card names the organelle, restates
   its job, and adds a fun fact — closing the loop immediately while attention is high.
4. **Misconception targeting via confusables.** As difficulty rises, distractors come from
   the answer's confusable set — the exact pairs students mix up:
   - Mitochondria ↔ Chloroplast (both energy organelles)
   - Ribosome ↔ ER ↔ Nucleus (the protein/DNA→protein pipeline)
   - Golgi ↔ ER ↔ Vacuole ↔ Lysosome (the endomembrane/packaging system)
   Distinguishing these subtler options is where the real understanding is built.

## Fun facts surfaced (the fact-card bank)

- **Nucleus** — keeps DNA safe behind a double membrane dotted with pores.
- **Mitochondria** — carry their own DNA, a clue they were once free-living bacteria.
- **Ribosome** — a single cell can run millions building proteins at once.
- **Chloroplast** — green because chlorophyll soaks up red and blue light.
- **Golgi** — works like a post office, addressing each protein for delivery.
- **Lysosome** — sacs of enzymes that recycle the cell's worn-out parts.
- **ER** — rough ER is studded with ribosomes; smooth ER builds fats.
- **Vacuole** — a plant cell's central vacuole can fill most of the cell with water.
- **Cell membrane** — a double layer of fat; some molecules slip through, others can't.

## Standards alignment (informal)

Middle-school life science (cell structure & function): "Develop and use a model to describe
the function of a cell as a whole and ways the parts of cells contribute to the function"
(NGSS MS-LS1-2). The endosymbiont fun facts (mitochondria/chloroplast own DNA) plant a seed
for later evolutionary biology.

---

## Assessment signal

The score is a proxy for mastery: speed-weighted correct answers under tightening time
pressure mean the player recalls the mapping fast, not by elimination. A high streak through
the confusable-heavy late game is the strongest signal of real understanding.
