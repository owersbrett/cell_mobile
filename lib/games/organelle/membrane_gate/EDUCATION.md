# EDUCATION.md — Membrane Gate

> The E in GAMES. What the player actually learns, and how the mechanic teaches
> it rather than telling it.

## Learning objective

Understand **selective permeability** — the central property of the cell
membrane — and the three ways things cross it:

1. **Simple diffusion** — small, nonpolar molecules (O₂, CO₂) pass directly
   through the lipid bilayer, down their concentration gradient.
2. **Osmosis** — water crosses through dedicated protein channels called
   **aquaporins** (a special, fast case of diffusion for water).
3. **Facilitated transport** — large or charged solutes (Na⁺, K⁺, glucose,
   amino acids) cannot cross the lipid alone; they require a matching **channel
   protein** (for ions) or **carrier protein** (for sugars/amino acids).

And the protective corollary: the membrane **excludes** what would harm the cell
— toxins, pathogens (viruses, bacteria), heavy metals, and waste.

## How the mechanic teaches it (not tells it)

- The membrane is **impermeable by default.** Nothing crosses unless the player
  acts — this is the felt meaning of "selective." Toxins that reach it *bounce*;
  the player sees the barrier work.
- Importing a wanted molecule **lights its specific route**: tap water and the
  aquaporin glows; tap glucose and the transporter glows; tap an ion and the ion
  channel glows; tap a gas and it slips straight through the lipid (no channel).
  The player links each molecule to its transport mechanism by repetition, not
  memorization.
- A small caption on each correct import names the route ("Aquaporin",
  "Ion channel", "Glucose transporter", "Free diffusion") — reinforcing the term
  at the moment of the action.
- Letting a toxin in (a mis-tap) costs points and dims the cell — the failure of
  selectivity has a visible, felt cost (the nucleus glow drops). Keeping toxins
  out keeps the cell thriving.

## Misconceptions corrected

- *"The membrane is just a passive wall."* → No: it actively, selectively
  transports specific solutes while blocking others.
- *"Everything diffuses through the membrane."* → Only small nonpolar molecules
  and (via aquaporins) water cross easily; ions and sugars need proteins.
- *"Bigger pressure pushes anything through."* → Charged and large molecules are
  excluded regardless; they need a matching protein.

## Curriculum hooks

- Maps to middle/high-school biology: cell transport, the fluid-mosaic model,
  diffusion vs. osmosis vs. facilitated transport, homeostasis.
- Cross-scale: the imported glucose and ions feed the Molecular-scale ATP and
  metabolism blocks; the membrane itself is the boundary the organelle-scale
  games live inside.

## One-sentence takeaway

> The cell membrane is selectively permeable: it lets the right molecules in
> through the right doors and keeps the wrong ones out.
