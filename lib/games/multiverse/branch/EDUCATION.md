# Branch — EDUCATION.md

> The E in GAMES. The lesson is **in the mechanic**, not bolted on as a quiz.

## Concept: the Many-Worlds Interpretation (MWI)
In the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics (Hugh Everett, 1957),
the wavefunction never "collapses." At every quantum decision the universe
**branches**: every possible outcome is realized, each on its own branch. There
is no special outcome that "really happens" and others that vanish — they all
happen, in parallel, decohered from one another.

## How the game *is* the lesson
- **Every choice splits the world in two, and both happen.** The tree literally
  forks at each generation and the abandoned branch is never deleted — it trails
  behind you, still splitting on its own. You *can't prune*; you can only
  navigate. That is the core MWI claim, made tactile.
- **Amplitude = the Born weight.** Each child shows a percentage. Larger
  amplitude branches carry more of the wavefunction's |ψ|² "measure." Riding the
  heavier branch (CLEAN) reflects the Born rule: most of *you* ends up where the
  amplitude is largest.
- **Decoherence.** The abandoned branches fan out and fade — low-amplitude
  worlds losing coherence with yours, dropping out of practical reach. You feel
  the irreversibility without it being a "loss."
- **No collapse, no punishment.** Picking the smaller branch still scores. The
  other outcome was just as real; you simply followed a thinner slice of your
  own measure. Nothing was destroyed — the game refuses to model collapse.

## The takeaway a player leaves with
> In many-worlds, every outcome is realized on some branch. You can't prune the
> tree — you can only choose which branch carries *you*, and the smart move is to
> ride where most of your amplitude already is.

## Honest caveats (for the curious)
MWI is one interpretation among several (Copenhagen, pilot-wave, objective
collapse). The "you steer which branch you're on" framing is a teaching
metaphor: in real MWI all branches contain a copy of you, and the Born rule
governs the *measure* of branches, not a literal steering wheel. The game trades
that subtlety for a graspable, hands-on intuition of branching + amplitude.
