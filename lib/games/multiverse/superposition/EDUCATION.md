# Superposition — EDUCATION.md

The education is **inside the mechanic** — you cannot score without using the physics.

## Concepts taught

### 1. Superposition
Before measurement, a qubit is not "up" or "down" — it is genuinely *both at once*, in a
weighted combination. In-game: the state vector sweeps the whole sphere and the cloud is
fuzzy. There is no definite answer until you measure.

### 2. The wavefunction
The combination is described by a wavefunction whose shape evolves over time. The visible
oscillation — `P(↑) = ½ + ½·sin(phase)` — *is* the wavefunction's probability evolving.
The odds shift continuously; they are not fixed.

### 3. Measurement & collapse
Observing a quantum system forces it into one definite state — it "collapses." In-game,
**MEASURE** snaps the sweeping vector to a single pole. You cannot peek without collapsing;
the act of measuring changes the system. This is the measurement problem, felt as a tap.

### 4. Probability amplitudes
The outcome is random but *weighted*: the larger the amplitude for a state, the more likely
that state is the result. Measure at `P(target) = 100%` and you are (nearly) certain to land
it; measure at the equator (50/50) and it is a true coin-flip. Your score equals the
probability you bet on — the game pays you for understanding the odds.

### 5. Joint probability multiplies (two-qubit)
For two independent qubits to *both* collapse to their targets, the probabilities multiply:
`P₁ × P₂`. Two 70% windows give only ~49% jointly — which is why the second qubit makes
perfect timing so demanding. Amplitudes compound; they do not average.

## Misconceptions corrected
- "The particle is secretly already up or down, we just don't know." — No: the probability
  bar genuinely sweeps; the outcome is undetermined until collapse.
- "If I wait long enough I'll always win." — No: at 50/50 it is a coin-flip no matter how
  long you stare. You must measure *at the right phase*.

## Discussion hook
Real quantum computers exploit exactly this: they hold qubits in superposition, steer the
amplitudes so the *right* answer becomes most probable, then measure. Superposition the game
is that loop in miniature — load the odds, then collapse.
