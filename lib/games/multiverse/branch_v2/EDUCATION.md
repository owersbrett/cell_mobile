# Branch v2 — Education (E)

## The lesson: the many-worlds interpretation
In the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, a measurement does not
"collapse" the wavefunction to one outcome. Instead the world **branches** — at
every quantum decision, *every* possible outcome happens, each in its own
worldline. You don't choose which outcome occurs; you only ever experience being
on **one** branch, while the others carry on, real but inaccessible to you.

## How the mechanic teaches it
- **Both outcomes happen.** Each fork always draws *two* children, and the one
  you don't ride doesn't vanish — it keeps splitting behind you (the dim
  `_abandonedSplit`) and fades out. You can never prune it; you can only navigate
  which world *you* continue. That is the core claim of many-worlds made
  tangible.
- **Amplitude / the Born rule.** Each child carries an **amplitude** — its weight
  |ψ|² — drawn as the node's radius and brightness and labelled as a %. The
  heavier child is the more probable world. Banking amplitude teaches that
  outcomes aren't equally weighted: probability is *amplitude*, and you read it
  off the geometry, not a coin flip.
- **Decoherence.** A world you stop steering loses coherence with yours — it
  decoheres into the background. If *you* fail to commit, **your own** measure
  decoheres: smeared across both worlds, you keep only the smaller amplitude.
  This embodies why the branches stop interfering and why a definite experience
  requires "selecting" a branch.
- **Coherence vs. interference (the resonance choice).** Riding the high-amplitude
  ("classical") path keeps you coherent; jumping the grain to grab a gold
  **resonance** node is a deliberate move to a lower-probability world for a
  payoff — and it breaks your coherent run. The tension between staying on the
  likely branch and diving for a rare resonance is the felt version of the
  amplitude/interference tradeoff.

## What a player should be able to say afterward
- "Every quantum choice splits the world and both outcomes really happen."
- "I can't delete the path I didn't take — it keeps splitting without me."
- "Bigger, brighter node = more probable world (that's its amplitude / |ψ|²)."
- "If I stop steering, my measurement smears across worlds (decoheres)."
- "Sometimes the unlikely world is worth diving for — but it costs coherence."

## Honesty notes (where it simplifies)
- Real amplitudes are complex numbers; we render only the squared magnitude
  (probability weight). No phase/interference math — the "resonance" is a game
  reward dramatizing the cost of leaving the high-probability branch, not a
  literal interference calculation.
- "Coherence as a score multiplier" is a gameplay metaphor for staying on one
  decohered worldline, not a measured physical quantity.
