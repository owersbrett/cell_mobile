# Food Web v2 — Education (E)

**Subject:** ecology — producers/consumers/decomposers, trophic levels, and the
flow (and loss) of energy up the food pyramid.

## What the player learns (by doing, not reading)
The teaching is the mechanic — preserved from v1, now felt continuously because
the energy is always draining and must always be replenished from below.

1. **Producers are the base.** Grass, clover and berries make their own energy
   (photosynthesis) — their rings never empty. Energy enters the web here and
   nowhere else.
2. **Energy flows UP, one level at a time.** You drag prey → predator. Dragging
   *down* fizzles ("energy flows UP") — energy never flows backward.
3. **No level-skipping.** Grass → Hawk fizzles ("no skipping — one level at a
   time"): a producer's energy reaches the apex only by passing up through every
   consumer between them.
4. **Energy is lost at each step (the 10% rule, now mechanical).** Feeding *up*
   costs a consumer more energy than it delivers, so the higher tiers are harder
   to keep alive — exactly why apex predators are few and starve first.
5. **Trophic levels are colour-coded** so the pyramid reads at a glance: green
   producers → gold primary → orange secondary → red apex.
6. **Decomposers close the loop.** Mushrooms accept energy from *any* organism
   (the purple recycle link), modelling how dead matter is broken down and
   returned to the soil.
7. **Chains = food chains.** A FULL CHAIN bonus fires only when one stream of
   energy travels the entire producer→primary→secondary→apex path — the player
   literally traces a complete food chain to score it.

## Vocabulary surfaced in play
producer · primary / secondary consumer · apex predator · decomposer ·
trophic level · energy flow · food web · food chain · 10% rule.

## Standards touchpoints
- NGSS **MS-LS2-3**: develop a model to describe the cycling of matter and flow
  of energy among living and nonliving parts of an ecosystem.
- NGSS **5-LS2-1**: model the movement of matter among plants, animals,
  decomposers, and the environment.
