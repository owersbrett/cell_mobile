# Food Web — Education (E)

**Subject:** ecology — producers/consumers/decomposers, trophic levels, and
energy flow up the food pyramid.

## What the player learns (by doing, not reading)
The teaching is the mechanic. To score, the player must build the *correct*
energy links, so they internalise the structure of an ecosystem:

1. **Producers are the base.** Grass, clover, acorns and berries make their own
   energy (photosynthesis). They are the only things with no incoming arrows —
   energy enters the web here.
2. **Energy flows UP, one level at a time.** You drag from prey to predator.
   Grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk. Dragging *down* (predator to
   prey) fizzles — energy never flows backward.
3. **No level-skipping.** Grass → fox fizzles: a producer's energy reaches a
   top predator only by passing through the consumers between them. This is how
   the game teaches discrete trophic levels.
4. **Trophic levels are colour-coded** so the pyramid reads at a glance:
   green producers → gold primary consumers → orange secondary → red apex.
5. **Decomposers close the loop.** Mushrooms and worms accept energy from *any*
   organism (the purple bonus link), modelling how dead matter is broken down
   and nutrients return to the soil for producers to reuse.
6. **The 10% rule (next pass).** Energy diminishes as it climbs — most is lost
   as heat at each transfer, which is why apex predators are few. The travelling
   energy pulses set up this intuition; a later pass thins them up the pyramid.

## Vocabulary surfaced in play
producer · primary / secondary consumer · apex predator · decomposer ·
trophic level · energy flow · food web · food chain.

## Standards touchpoints
- NGSS **MS-LS2-3**: develop a model to describe the cycling of matter and flow
  of energy among living and nonliving parts of an ecosystem.
- NGSS **5-LS2-1**: model the movement of matter among plants, animals,
  decomposers, and the environment.
