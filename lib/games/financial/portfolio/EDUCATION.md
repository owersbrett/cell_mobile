# EDUCATION.md — Portfolio (Financial scale)

> The educational payload of the `portfolio` game: **stocks/ETFs and diversification**. The game is the
> vehicle; this is what a player should walk away understanding. The lesson is taught by *feel* first —
> you watch your line ride between a wild stock and a smooth basket — and named second.

- **Scale (cell):** financial
- **Game on this scale:** `portfolio` (`PortfolioGame`). Companion: `market_trader` (single-asset
  timing/order-book). Portfolio is the **allocation/risk** half; Market Trader is the **timing** half.

---

## The one idea: don't put all your eggs in one basket

A **stock** is a share of one company. Its price can climb fast — and fall just as fast on one bad day
(an earnings miss, a sector crash). Owning only one stock means your entire outcome rides on that one
company's luck.

**Diversification** is spreading your money across many different holdings. When one zigs and another
zags, the swings partly cancel out. You give up a little of the best-case moonshot, but you remove the
worst-case wipeout. Over many shocks, the steadier book wins more reliably.

---

## Concepts the game makes concrete

### 1. Volatility vs. return
- **Return** = how much your money grows. **Volatility** = how wildly it swings on the way there.
- A single stock in the game has **high volatility**: huge up days, brutal down days. The **YOU** line
  vs the **1 STOCK** reference line shows it directly — the single-stock line whips up and down.
- Higher potential return usually comes bundled with higher volatility. The skill is deciding **how
  much swing you can stomach** for the climb you want.

### 2. Why a basket is smoother (the math, felt)
- The **TUBR** ETF in the game moves like a blend of all four sectors. When one sector crashes, the
  basket only feels about **a quarter** of it — the other three cushion the fall.
- This is real: averaging across uncorrelated holdings shrinks the swings without shrinking the
  long-run drift much. The **BASKET** line on the chart is visibly calmer than the **1 STOCK** line.

### 3. What an ETF / index fund actually is
- An **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)** or **index fund** is a single thing you can buy that *already holds
  many companies inside it*. Buying one share of an index ETF = instantly owning a slice of dozens or
  hundreds of stocks.
- In the game, **ALL-IN ETF** is one tap that turns your whole book diversified. In the real world,
  that's why broad index funds are the default recommendation for most people: built-in diversification,
  no stock-picking required.

### 4. Concentration risk and correlation
- **Concentration risk:** the more of your money sits in one stock (or one sector), the more a single
  bad event can ruin you. The **RISK SPREAD** gauge shows this live — CONCENTRATED (red) → DIVERSIFIED
  (green).
- **Correlation:** holdings that tend to move *together* don't diversify you much. Late in the game,
  two sectors crash **at the same time** (a correlated crash) — spreading across things that all fall
  together doesn't save you. True safety comes from holdings that *don't* move in lockstep (and from the
  steadier basket).

### 5. Risk-adjusted thinking
- Winning isn't "pick the stock that goes up most." It's **growing your value while surviving the
  shocks**. A score that ends high *and* never cratered beats a lucky spike that got wiped — which is
  why the streak award rewards crashes weathered with a small drawdown.

---

## Misconceptions this corrects

| Misconception | What the game shows |
|---|---|
| "Diversification just means owning lots of stuff." | It means owning stuff that *doesn't crash together* — correlated holdings don't help. |
| "Playing it safe means giving up returns." | The basket still climbs; it mostly gives up the *wild swings*, not the growth. |
| "Pick the winner and go all-in." | Drifts reshuffle and shocks are random — concentration is a gamble that can get wiped. |
| "An ETF is complicated." | It's one purchase that holds many companies — instant diversification. |

---

## One-line takeaway
**Spreading a fixed budget across uncorrelated holdings (or a single index ETF) keeps the climb while
cutting the crash — that's diversification, and it's why "don't put all your eggs in one basket" is the
first rule of investing.**
