## The concept — the reflex arc
A reflex is an involuntary, near-instant response to a stimulus, routed through the spinal cord
**without waiting for the brain**. The five stages are exactly the five nodes on screen:

| Stage | On-screen node | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Stimulus** | Receptor (flash) | A change is detected — a hot stove, a tap on the tendon. |
| **Sensory neuron (afferent)** | Receptor → Spinal cord (blue limb) | Carries the signal *toward* the CNS. |
| **CNS / integration** | Spinal cord (lime hub) | An interneuron relays straight to the motor neuron. |
| **Motor neuron (efferent)** | Spinal cord → Muscle (gold limb) | Carries the command *away* to the effector. |
| **Response** | Muscle (contracts) | The effector acts — the hand withdraws. |

## Why it bypasses the brain (load-bearing lesson — kept from v1)
The brain is drawn **faded, above the cord, on a dotted "bypassed" link.** A reflex resolves at the
spinal cord, so the signal travels a far shorter loop than to the brain and back — fewer synapses +
less distance = milliseconds saved. The **damage arc** dramatises it: react before it fills, or the
slow path costs you. The **brain decoy** (a false flash you must ignore) reinforces that the reflex
is receptor-driven, bottom-up.

## New lesson in v2 — the reflex is graded and discriminating (go / no-go)
A real nervous system does not fire withdrawal at *every* skin contact — a gentle touch should NOT
yank your hand away, or you could never hold anything. The cue colour models this:
- **ORANGE (GO)** = a **noxious / threatening** stimulus → the withdrawal reflex *should* fire. Tap.
- **TEAL (NO-GO)** = a **benign** touch → the response must be **inhibited**. Hold.

This is a **go/no-go task**, a classic of motor-control neuroscience used to study **response
inhibition**. Embedding it teaches three real ideas at once:
- **Discrimination:** the receptor evaluates *what kind* of stimulus arrived, not merely *that* one did.
- **Inhibition is active work.** Holding still on a NO-GO — especially when most trials are GO and you
  are primed to tap — is a genuine effort, and the game scores it as a win (the inhibition reward).
- **Speed vs judgement trade-off.** Pure twitch (tap everything fast) now *loses*, because it misfires
  on NO-GO cues. You must perceive, decide, and only then react — which is exactly how a healthy
  reflex behaves: fast, but not indiscriminate.

## How the teaching is embedded (not bolted on)
- **You can't score without tracing the real circuit.** Every GO hit plays the impulse afferent →
  CNS → efferent → effector, in order — repetition burns the sequence in.
- **A correct HOLD shows the muscle staying calm** — visual proof that "no response" is the correct
  output for a benign cue.
- **The CHARGE multiplier rewards consistent discrimination**, not raw speed — mirroring that a
  reliable, well-regulated reflex is more valuable than a twitchy one.
- **Reaction-time readout (ms) + a best-of-round ghost** make "reflexes are fast" concrete and personal.

## Real-world anchors
- **Patellar (knee-jerk) reflex** — the classic monosynaptic stretch reflex a doctor tests.
- **Withdrawal reflex** — yanking a hand off a hot surface before you consciously feel pain.
- **Go/no-go inhibition** — stopping yourself from grabbing a pan handle you just realised is hot.

## Potato angle
Light — this is a human-body story. Optional nod: plants have no nerves, so a potato's "responses"
(tropisms, turgor) are chemical and slow, and certainly never *discriminate* in milliseconds. The
contrast underlines what a discriminating nervous system buys an animal. Keep the focus on the arc.
