# EDUCATION.md — Digest

> The educational component of the **Digest** game (the **E** in GAMES). The learning is delivered
> *through the mechanic* — you can't play without rehearsing the biology.

## Learning objectives
A player who finishes a few rounds can:
1. **Recall the order of the digestive tract:** MOUTH → ESOPHAGUS → STOMACH → SMALL INTESTINE →
   LARGE INTESTINE. The game forces left-to-right routing in exactly this sequence.
2. **Match each organ to its job:** mouth **chews** (mechanical digestion), esophagus **swallows**
   (peristalsis), stomach **churns** (acid + muscle → chyme), small intestine **absorbs nutrients**,
   large intestine **absorbs water**.
3. **Name where absorption happens** — nutrient absorption in the **small intestine** and water
   absorption in the **large intestine** are the highest-scoring actions, the two facts students most
   often confuse. The score gradient teaches it.
4. **Understand digestion as a pipeline** — each stage must finish and the next must be clear before
   food moves on.
5. **Explain the reflexes and hazards of eating:** thorough **chewing**, why we **spit out** spoiled
   food, what a **choke** is and how the **epiglottis** normally prevents it, why **water** relieves
   **capsaicin** heat, and why the body periodically **eliminates** waste.

## How the mechanics teach (no separate quiz)

### Mechanical digestion — chew depth (1–3 taps)
Tough foods (corn, greens) need more chews than soft foods; the pips on the morsel show the work
remaining. This models **mechanical digestion**: the mouth physically breaks food down before chemical
digestion continues. Chewing has a rhythm (a short cooldown) because real mastication takes time — you
can't swallow a whole bite at once. Well-chewed food becomes a **bolus**, then **chyme** after the
stomach.

### Spoiled food — spit or suffer
Some morsels are **bad** (spoiled/contaminated). Spitting them at the mouth is the body's first line of
defense (disgust response, gag reflex). **Swallowing** bad food makes the body **sick** and slows the
whole tract — a stand-in for food-borne illness disrupting digestion. Lesson: your senses screen food
*before* it goes down; when in doubt, don't swallow it.

### Choking & the epiglottis
A swallow can jam — the game's **CHOKE** emergency. In reality the **epiglottis** flaps over the airway
during a normal swallow so food goes down the esophagus, not the trachea. When something goes down "the
wrong pipe," the body coughs forcefully to dislodge it (the game's **mash**), and a sip of **water**
helps wash the bolus down afterward. The alarm teaches that swallowing is a coordinated reflex, not
automatic safety.

### Spice & capsaicin — why water "doesn't work" but we still reach for it
Spicy morsels trigger a **burn** the moment they hit the tongue. Chili heat comes from **capsaicin**,
which activates the same heat/pain receptor (TRPV1) as actual high temperature — the tongue reports
"hot" though nothing is burning. The game rewards grabbing **water** to douse it fast (relief +
gameplay), while nodding to the real subtlety that capsaicin is oil-based; the point students take is
**the sensation is chemical, and you respond immediately.**

### Peristalsis & the pipeline
Food only ever advances forward, one organ at a time — mirroring **peristalsis**, the muscular waves
(especially in the esophagus) that push the bolus along. The "one bolus per organ, clear the front
first" rule shows the tract processing a meal **in stages**, not all at once.

### Elimination — the flush
After enough churn cycles the tract fills, and a **flush** clears it for a bonus. This models
**elimination**: the large intestine compacts what's left after water reabsorption and the body
periodically expels waste, freeing the system to process the next meal.

### Absorption emphasis
Scoring weight + the "+NUTRIENTS" / "+WATER" popups foreground the two absorption sites (small and large
intestine) — the exam-critical facts.

## Facts surfaced in play
- The **small intestine** is where ~90% of nutrient absorption occurs; the **large intestine** mainly
  reabsorbs **water** and salts.
- The **esophagus** uses **peristalsis** to transport the bolus — no absorption there.
- A chewed, churned mass becomes **chyme** before the small intestine.
- The **epiglottis** protects the airway during swallowing; choking is that safeguard failing.
- **Capsaicin** creates heat by triggering heat/pain receptors on the tongue — a chemical, not thermal,
  burn.

## Standards alignment (informal)
Middle-school life science: human body systems — structure and function of the digestive system;
sequence of organs; mechanical vs. chemical digestion; sites of absorption; protective reflexes
(chewing, gag, cough); the sensory basis of taste/irritants.
