# EDUCATION.md — Vocab Engine (+ Finance Lingo)

> What this module teaches, and **how** it teaches it. The headline: a single engine teaches
> **domain vocabulary through active recall**, and the *same* engine powers a vocab game for **every
> scale** — just point it at a different `VocabBank`.

---

## The pedagogy: active recall, not passive reading

The engine isn't a glossary you scroll. It's a **retrieval-practice** loop. Each round shows a
definition and forces you to *produce* the matching term from four options under time pressure.
Pulling a fact *out* of memory (recall) builds far stronger, longer-lasting knowledge than reading
the same fact *in* (recognition). The engine leans on three well-studied learning mechanisms:

1. **Active recall.** Every question is a retrieval attempt — the single most effective study action
   in the research literature.
2. **The testing effect.** Being quizzed on material (not just re-reading it) measurably improves
   later retention. A 60 s round is ~15–18 micro-tests.
3. **Immediate feedback + spacing.** After every answer a **reinforcement card** shows the term, its
   definition, and a one-line note — closing the loop while the attempt is fresh. The no-repeat
   shuffle re-presents terms across runs, approximating spaced repetition.

The **speed incentive** (faster = more points, and the window tightens as the round runs) pushes
toward **fluency** — the goal isn't just knowing a term, it's recognizing it *fast*, the way a
practitioner does.

---

## The engine is the lesson-delivery system for the whole catalog

This is the part worth underlining: **the educational component is reusable.** Vocabulary is the
entry fee to every domain — you can't reason about a field whose words you don't hold. So instead of
hand-building a bespoke quiz per scale, this module ships **one engine** and treats each scale's
vocabulary as **data** (`VocabBank`). The same active-recall loop that teaches finance terms teaches
cell-biology terms, atomic terms, cosmology terms — for the cost of a word list.

> One engine, many curricula. A new scale's vocab lesson = a new `VocabBank`, no new code.

That makes vocabulary **cheap to add everywhere**, which is exactly what you want for a learning game
that spans 20+ scales.

## First instance — Finance Lingo (financial scale)

`kFinanceVocab` teaches **~30 core market-literacy terms** — the words you need before any other
financial reasoning makes sense. They cluster into a few families:

- **Risk & price behavior:** liquidity, volatility, leverage, diversification, hedge, arbitrage.
- **Returns & income:** yield, dividend, capital gain, compound interest, interest rate.
- **Instruments:** stock/equity, bond, ETF, index fund, blue chip.
- **Valuation:** P/E ratio, market cap, bid-ask spread.
- **Market states & events:** bull market, bear market, recession, inflation, IPO, stock split,
  short selling.
- **Accounting basics:** asset, liability, capital, portfolio.

Each carries a plain-English definition (the prompt), three plausible same-domain distractors (so a
wrong tap is a *real* confusion to clear up — e.g. liquidity vs. solvency, bull vs. bear, asset vs.
liability), and a one-line `note` that adds an example or flags the common mix-up. By the end of a
few rounds, a player has drilled the working vocabulary of personal finance and markets — the literal
foundation the rest of the financial scale builds on.

## Association verdict

**Strong, foundational tie to the financial scale.** The financial scale is where the app reasons
about markets, value, and exchange. You cannot operate there without its vocabulary — so a
vocabulary game is the natural *first rung* of that scale's education. And because the engine is
generic, the same verdict will hold the moment a `VocabBank` is added for any other scale: vocabulary
is foundational everywhere, and this engine delivers it everywhere the same way.
