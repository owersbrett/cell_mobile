# EDUCATION.md — Constants (the **E** in GAMES)

**Topic:** The fine-tuning of the physical constants — why our universe appears
"just right" for stars, atoms and chemistry.

## The core idea
A handful of dimensionless numbers fix the character of physics. If they were
even slightly different, no complex structure — and so no life — could form. The
game makes this tangible: each constant is a dial with a *narrow habitable band*,
and the universe preview shows you exactly how reality breaks when you leave it.

## Education is IN the mechanic
You don't read facts — you *feel* the cliff edges. Every out-of-band state
produces a real, named consequence on screen:

| Dial | What it controls | Push it out → what really happens |
|------|------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **G** — gravity strength | how strongly mass attracts mass | **Too strong:** stars burn out / collapse almost instantly — no stable, long-lived suns. **Too weak:** gas never clumps into galaxies or stars at all. |
| **S** — strong nuclear force | binds protons & neutrons into nuclei | **Off / too weak:** no nuclei beyond hydrogen → no chemistry. **Too strong:** even lone protons fuse, so no free hydrogen → no water, no stars as we know them. |
| **Λ** — cosmological constant | the energy density driving cosmic expansion | **Too high:** space expands so fast matter never gravitationally assembles — the cosmos rips apart. **Too low (negative):** the universe recollapses before structure forms. (Real Λ is famously tiny yet non-zero.) |
| **μ** — electron/proton mass ratio | sets atomic & molecular structure | Shift it and atoms/molecules lose the stability that all of chemistry — and biology — depends on. |

## The takeaway (the "anthropic" question)
The bands really are narrow: small relative changes in these constants give a
sterile universe. Why ours sits in the life-permitting sweet spot is an open
question with three classic stances — **chance**, a **multiverse** (many
universes with different constants, we observe a habitable one), or **deeper
necessity** (some not-yet-known theory forces these values). The game stays
agnostic; it just lets you *experience* how special "habitable" is by trying,
and failing, to hold all the dials at once.

## What a player should leave knowing
1. A few fundamental constants jointly decide whether stars, atoms and chemistry
   can exist.
2. The "habitable" range for each is narrow, and the constraints interact — you
   must satisfy them **simultaneously**.
3. "Fine-tuning" names this observation; the multiverse and anthropic reasoning
   are the leading attempts to explain it.

## Accelerates with skill
Bands narrow and drift speeds up as the round goes — a felt analogue for *how
tight* the real constraints are. The better you get, the more sharply you sense
how little slack reality actually has.
