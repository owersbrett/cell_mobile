# Powers of Ten v2 — Education (E)

## The lesson: orders of magnitude on a logarithmic scale
The universe spans dozens of powers of ten — from a proton (~10⁻¹⁵ m) to the
observable universe (~10²⁷ m), from an electron's mass to a galaxy's, from a
yoctosecond to the age of the cosmos. A **logarithmic ladder** is how scientists
hold that whole range in one view: each step up is ×10, not +1. To place a thing
correctly you **interpolate between anchors you know** — that is exactly the
skill this game drills.

## How the mechanic teaches it
- **Place between anchors.** The ladder prints faint anchors (atom 10⁻¹⁰ m,
  human 10⁰, Earth 10⁷, galaxy 10²¹; electron / Sun for mass; ns / year / age of
  universe for time). You drop the thing *relative* to those — the core act of
  estimating a magnitude.
- **Orders of magnitude = repeated multiplication.** Every item is phrased as a
  **factor on a known anchor**: "a line of 12,000 sand grains", "3× the Sun's
  mass", "500 human lifetimes". To place it you multiply the anchor's exponent by
  the factor — i.e. you *add logs*. The randomized factor forces this reasoning
  every single time; you can't memorize a list.
- **The reveal closes the loop.** On each drop the truth slides home from where
  you guessed (the error band shows how many decades off), and the value
  (`≈ 3.2 × 10⁷ s`) plus a one-line fact appears — turning a guess into a learned
  magnitude.
- **Three quantities, one idea.** Size (meters), mass (kilograms) and time
  (seconds) rotate in as you climb, so the log-scale intuition generalizes across
  physical dimensions, not just length.

## What a player should be able to say afterward
- "A logarithmic scale steps by ×10, so I place things between anchors I know."
- "To find where '3× the Sun's mass' sits, I add log10(3) to the Sun's exponent."
- "Size, mass and time all span dozens of orders of magnitude."
- "Being one decade off means I was 10× wrong — magnitude matters more than the
  leading digit."

## Honesty notes (where it simplifies)
- Anchor values are rounded (Sun ≈ 1.39M km wide, ≈ 2 × 10³⁰ kg; a heartbeat
  ≈ 0.85 s). The point is the *order of magnitude*, not a precise measurement.
- Factors are linear scalings of a single base unit (a line of N grains, N
  elephants). Real objects don't always scale linearly with count (e.g. volume
  goes as the cube of length) — the game keeps factors linear so the lesson stays
  "multiply the anchor," cleanly readable on a log ladder.
