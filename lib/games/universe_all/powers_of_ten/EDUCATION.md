# Powers of Ten — EDUCATION.md

## Learning objective
Build intuition for **orders of magnitude** and the staggering range of scales in
nature — from the quantum to the cosmic — the classic "powers of ten" zoom. By
the end of a run the player should be able to roughly place unfamiliar things on a
logarithmic ladder by interpolating between anchors they know.

## Education is IN the mechanic
- **The action is the lesson.** Placing a thing on a log ladder *is* estimating
  its order of magnitude. You cannot play without reasoning about scale.
- **Anchors as scaffold.** Each ladder prints named reference points — atom
  (10⁻¹⁰ m), human (10⁰ m), Earth (10⁷ m), galaxy (10²¹ m) — so the player learns
  by interpolation: "a virus is between an atom and me, closer to the atom."
- **Reveal teaches the truth.** Every drop shows the true magnitude, the
  real-world value with units (≈ 100 nm, 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, 13.8 billion yr), the
  gap in *decades*, and a one-line fact. Error is expressed in powers of ten, the
  unit of the concept itself.
- **Logarithmic thinking.** Because the ladder is logarithmic, the player
  internalizes that each step is ×10 — the only way to fit a quark and the
  universe on one screen.

## Concepts covered
- **Scale & magnitude:** nanometers → meters → light-years → the observable
  universe (~10²⁷ m); the human is near the geometric middle of the size range.
- **Three quantities, same vastness:** size (m), mass (kg), and time (s) each
  span dozens of decades — reinforcing that "orders of magnitude" is a general
  tool, not a size-only trick.
- **Reference values worth knowing:** atom 10⁻¹⁰ m, virus 10⁻⁷ m, Earth's
  diameter 10⁷ m, the Sun 10⁹ m and 2 × 10³⁰ kg, Earth's mass 6 × 10²⁴ kg,
  a year 3 × 10⁷ s, the universe's age 4 × 10¹⁷ s.

## How difficulty deepens the learning
Tolerances tighten and the thing pool grows trickier, pushing the player from
"is this big or small?" to precise placement (DNA at 2 nm vs. a virus at 100 nm;
an astronomical unit vs. the whole solar system). New quantities (mass, then time)
rotate in once size is intuitive, transferring the skill across domains.

## Discussion prompts (for facilitated sessions)
- Why is a human roughly in the middle of the size ladder, but nowhere near the
  middle of the mass or time ladders?
- A virus is 1,000× bigger than an atom but 1,000× smaller than an ant — how many
  decades is each gap?
- The observable universe is ~10²⁷ m; a proton is ~10⁻¹⁵ m. How many powers of
  ten separate them?
