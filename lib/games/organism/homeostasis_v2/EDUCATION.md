# Homeostasis v2 — Education (E)

## Core concept
**Homeostasis** is the maintenance of a stable internal environment despite a
changing outside world. The body holds variables — temperature, water balance,
blood glucose, blood oxygen, pH — within narrow safe ranges around a **set
point**.

## The mechanism: negative feedback
The game *is* a negative-feedback loop. A negative-feedback loop has three parts:
1. **Receptor / sensor** — detects a deviation from the set point (you reading
   the needle leave the green band).
2. **Control center** — decides the response (you choosing ▼ or ▲).
3. **Effector** — carries out the correction that **opposes** the change (the tap).

"Negative" means the response *counteracts* the deviation, pulling the value back
toward the set point — never amplifying it. The v2 **whiplash penalty** teaches
this directly: if you slam the needle clean through the set point and out the
opposite side, you are no longer doing negative feedback — you have **over-
corrected**, and the body pays for it. Real feedback nudges toward balance; it
does not oscillate wildly. Holding several gauges at once shows the body runs many
of these loops in parallel, all the time.

## What each control models (real physiology)
- **Temperature** — hypothalamic thermostat. Too hot → **sweating /
  vasodilation**; too cold → **shivering / vasoconstriction**.
- **Water** — osmoregulation by the kidneys + ADH. Excess → **excrete (urine)**;
  deficit → **thirst / drink** and water reabsorption.
- **Blood sugar** — pancreatic islets. High glucose → **insulin** (cells take up
  glucose, liver stores glycogen); low glucose → **glucagon** (liver releases
  glucose). A classic antagonistic hormone pair.
- **Oxygen / CO₂** — respiratory drive. Low O₂ (or high CO₂) → **breathe faster /
  deeper**; the body rarely needs to slow breathing, modeled here as **exhale**.

## Why the "shocks" matter
EXERCISE, a BIG MEAL, a COLD SNAP, DEHYDRATION, or THIN AIR are real
**perturbations**. Homeostasis is not "nothing changes" — it is the body
constantly correcting *back* after the world pushes it. Feeling several shocks
land at once teaches that these loops compete for attention but each one keeps
defending its own variable.

## Why the vital sign races
The whole-screen vignette and the heartbeat **speed up as the body destabilises**.
Physiologically, losing homeostatic control (fever, hypoglycaemia, hypoxia) drives
compensatory responses — a racing heart is the body fighting to restore balance.
The faster, redder screen is that fight made visible.

## Takeaways a player should leave with
- The body actively *defends* set points; stability is work, not stillness.
- Negative feedback = response **opposes** deviation, and **over-correcting is its
  own failure** (the whiplash).
- Insulin/glucagon, sweat/shiver, drink/excrete are **opposing pairs** — the
  control center picks the one that counteracts the current error.
- Many loops run in parallel; that parallelism is what "staying alive" feels like.
