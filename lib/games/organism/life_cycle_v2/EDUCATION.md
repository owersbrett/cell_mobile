# EDUCATION.md — Life Cycle v2

> The **E** in GAMES. The learning is IN the mechanic: to score you must know the ORDER of an
> organism's life cycle — and v2 makes that even more direct than the original. You don't pick a word
> off a card; you point at the correct NEXT node on the wheel and then WALK the entire cycle, so the
> full sequence is reconstructed by hand every organism. The metamorphosis-type chip + the fact ribbon
> teach WHY the order is what it is, without ever stopping play.

## Learning objectives
A player who does well can:
1. **Sequence a life cycle** — point to what stage comes next for many organisms (egg → larva → pupa →
   adult; egg → tadpole → froglet → frog; seed → seedling → plant → flower → seed; etc.). Because the
   nodes are spatially SHUFFLED, recognising a clockwise neighbour does not help — only knowing the
   order does.
2. **Classify metamorphosis type** — tell **complete** (egg → larva → pupa → adult, a total rebuild)
   from **incomplete** (egg → nymph → adult, a tiny copy that just grows) from **direct development**
   (no metamorphosis) from a **plant life cycle**. The type chip is always on screen and colour-codes
   the wheel.
3. **Recognise that a life cycle is a CYCLE** — walking a full lap returns you to the start node, so
   the adult/seed visibly produces the next generation rather than ending at "adult".

## The four metamorphosis types (the core concept)
| Type | Pattern | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| **Complete metamorphosis** | egg → larva → **pupa** → adult; larva looks nothing like the adult | butterfly, ladybug, bee, mosquito |
| **Incomplete metamorphosis** | egg → **nymph** → adult; no pupa; nymph is a small wingless adult | grasshopper, dragonfly, cockroach |
| **Direct development** | egg → young → adult; the young is just a small adult | chicken, sea turtle |
| **Plant life cycle** | seed → seedling → plant → flower → (seed) | sunflower, apple tree, potato |

The trickiest rounds (tier 2) deliberately put a `larva` cycle next to a `nymph` cycle, so the
metamorphosis TYPE is the only way to be sure of the order.

## How v2 keeps the teaching while fixing the pace
- **No blocking flare.** The original froze ~2.0s on a fact after EVERY answer. v2 moves the fact into
  a persistent bottom ribbon that updates per organism and never gates the next tap — correct answers
  advance instantly. Teaching is ambient, not a stop sign.
- **Dwell only on a miss.** When you tap the wrong node (or let the timer arc close), the CORRECT node
  flashes green for ~0.8s — a targeted reveal exactly when you got the order wrong, which is when the
  correction lands hardest.
- **The path builds visibly.** Visited nodes keep a faint green check ring, so a completed lap draws
  the whole sequence on screen — a reinforcement the flat card quiz could never show.

## Facts surfaced in play (the ribbon)
Each organism carries a one-line fact shown while you walk its cycle — e.g.:
- *Inside the chrysalis the caterpillar dissolves into a soup and rebuilds as a butterfly.*
- *A grasshopper nymph is a tiny wingless copy of the adult — no pupa.*
- *Mosquito larvae and pupae both live in water; only the adult flies.*
- *A chick hatches looking like a tiny adult — no metamorphosis.*

## Potato / Potatuhs through-line
The **Potato** cycle (seed potato → sprout → plant → flower → tuber → seed potato) is the organism-
scale headliner: a potato is an organ of the plant, but an organism once it grows an eye and is
planted — the tuber IS the next seed. Walking that lap connects "what is a life cycle" to "what counts
as an organism".

## Misconceptions it corrects
- "All baby animals look like their parents" — false for complete/incomplete metamorphosis.
- "Metamorphosis means growing wings/legs" — the real divide is whether there's a **pupa** stage.
- "A life cycle ends at the adult" — it loops; the lap returns to the start node.
- "Tadpoles and froglets are different species" — same animal, different life stage.
