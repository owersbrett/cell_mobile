# EDUCATION.md — Life Cycle

> The **E** in GAMES. The learning is IN the mechanic: to score you must know the ORDER of an
> organism's life cycle, and the metamorphosis-type chip + the turning wheel + the fact flare teach
> WHY that order is what it is. Difficulty accelerates the lesson — easy distinct cycles first, then
> confusable larvae/nymphs where the metamorphosis TYPE is the only way to get the order right.

## Learning objectives
A player who does well can:
1. **Sequence a life cycle** — state what stage comes next for many organisms (egg → larva → pupa →
   adult; egg → tadpole → froglet → frog; seed → seedling → plant → flower → seed; etc.).
2. **Classify metamorphosis type** — tell **complete** (egg → larva → pupa → adult, a total rebuild)
   from **incomplete** (egg → nymph → adult, a tiny copy that just grows) from **direct development**
   (no metamorphosis at all) from a **plant life cycle**.
3. **Recognise that a life cycle is a CYCLE** — the adult/seed produces the next generation, so the
   stages loop forever rather than ending at "adult".

## The four metamorphosis types (the core concept)
| Type | Pattern | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| **Complete metamorphosis** | egg → larva → **pupa** → adult; larva looks nothing like the adult | butterfly, ladybug, bee, mosquito |
| **Incomplete metamorphosis** | egg → **nymph** → adult; no pupa; nymph is a small wingless adult | grasshopper, dragonfly, cockroach |
| **Direct development** | egg → young → adult; the young is just a small adult | chicken, sea turtle |
| **Plant life cycle** | seed → seedling → plant → flower → (seed) | sunflower, apple tree, potato |

The metamorphosis-type **chip is always on screen**, so over a run the player repeatedly pairs an
organism with its type — and the trickiest rounds deliberately put a `larva` next to a `nymph` so the
type is the tiebreaker.

## Facts surfaced in play (the flare)
Every organism carries a one-line fact shown after each answer — e.g.:
- *Inside the chrysalis the caterpillar dissolves into a soup and rebuilds as a butterfly.*
- *A grasshopper nymph is a tiny wingless copy of the adult — no pupa.*
- *Mosquito larvae and pupae both live in water; only the adult flies.*
- *A chick hatches looking like a tiny adult — no metamorphosis.*

## Potato / Potatuhs through-line
The **Potato** cycle (seed potato → sprout → plant → flower → tuber → seed potato) ties into the
organism-scale headliner: **a potato is an organ of the plant, but an organism once it grows an eye
and is planted — the tuber IS the next seed.** This connects "what is a life cycle" to "what counts as
an organism", the organism scale's central question.

## Misconceptions it corrects
- "All baby animals look like their parents" — false for complete/incomplete metamorphosis.
- "Metamorphosis means growing wings/legs" — the real divide is whether there's a **pupa** stage.
- "A life cycle ends at the adult" — it loops; adults make the next eggs/seeds.
- "Tadpoles and froglets are different species" — same animal, different life stage.
