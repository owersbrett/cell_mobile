# Electron Shells v2 — Education (E)

## The lesson, in the mechanic
Electrons occupy **shells** (energy levels) around the nucleus, and they fill
from the inside out. The game makes you *do* exactly that: you cannot seat an
electron in L until K is full, and the tray electrons are labelled by the shell
they belong to — so seating an atom IS performing its electron configuration.

## Concepts taught
- **Electron shells / energy levels (K, L, M, N).** Drawn as concentric rings.
  Each electron carries its level; you sort by level.
- **Shell capacity (the 2-8-8-8 model).** K holds 2, L/M/N hold 8 in this
  teaching model. Ghost slots show every seat, so the capacity is visible.
- **Inside-out (Aufbau) filling.** The active shell is always the lowest
  unfilled one. Try to fill outward early and the game tells you `FILL K FIRST`.
- **The octet / duet rule.** A full outer shell (8, or 2 for K) is stable —
  flagged `OCTET!` / `DUET!`. Atoms whose neutral configuration ends on a full
  shell are **noble gases** (He, Ne, Ar), tagged `NOBLE — FULL OCTET` on
  completion. This is *why* atoms react: to reach a full outer shell.
- **Atomic number = electron count (neutral atom).** The nucleus shows `Z` (the
  proton count); a neutral atom seats exactly `Z` electrons.
- **Electron configuration notation.** The panel shows the target as `2-8-1`
  (sodium) — the same per-shell counts you build.

## Accuracy notes
- Configurations for the first 20 elements (H→Ca) are exact.
- The **2-8-8-8** capacities are the standard introductory shell model. Real
  subshell capacities (M holds 18, etc.) are a later refinement; this game
  teaches the periodic-table-friendly version used to introduce the octet rule.

## Why this is fun *and* educational
The decision the player makes every second — "which electron belongs in the
shell I must fill next" — is the chemistry. There's no busywork wrapped around a
fact; the fact is the move. The escalating bonus rewards completing bigger,
multi-shell atoms, which is exactly where the configuration skill matters most.
