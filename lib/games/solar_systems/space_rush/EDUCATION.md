# EDUCATION.md — Space Rush

> The **E** in GAMES. What a player actually learns. The teaching is the mechanic: each microgame *is*
> the behavior of one solar-system body, and a one-line fact rides on every prompt card.

## Big idea
The solar system is not one kind of thing — it's a **menagerie**. A star, rocky and gas-giant planets,
moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets each move and behave by different rules. Space Rush makes
you *do* the defining behavior of each, one body at a time, so the variety lands as muscle memory, not
a list.

## Per-microgame concept

### DODGE! — the asteroid belt
Between Mars and Jupiter lies a belt of millions of rocky bodies, the rubble that never formed a planet
(Jupiter's gravity kept stirring it up). Despite the count, the belt is mostly **empty space** — the
rocks are so far apart that spacecraft fly straight through. *Teaches: asteroids are leftover building
blocks, spread thin.*

### CATCH! — a comet's tail
Comets are icy "dirty snowballs" from the cold outer system. As one nears the Sun, its ice **sublimates**
(turns straight to gas), releasing dust that forms a glowing tail. The tail is pushed by sunlight and
the solar wind, so it always points **away from the Sun** — even as the comet heads back out. *Teaches:
comets shed dust/gas; tails point away from the Sun.*

### LAND! — the Moon
The Moon has about **1/6 of Earth's gravity** and essentially **no atmosphere**. With no air, a parachute
does nothing — a lander must brake with **rocket thrust** all the way down, balancing gravity against the
engine to touch down softly. *Teaches: low gravity + no air → powered landings.*

### SPIN! — a gas giant
Jupiter and the other gas giants have **no solid surface** and spin astonishingly fast — Jupiter's day is
under **10 hours**. That rapid rotation flings its equator outward (it's visibly **oblate** / squashed)
and shears its atmosphere into banded jet streams and long-lived storms like the Great Red Spot.
*Teaches: gas giants rotate fastest; rotation drives their bands and bulge.*

### SORT! — dwarf planets
A **dwarf planet** (Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Haumea, Makemake) is round from its own gravity but **hasn't
cleared its orbit** of other debris — the rule that separates it from the eight planets. They're small,
icy, and often far out in the Kuiper Belt. *Teaches: what makes a dwarf planet different from a planet.*

### TILT! — axial tilt and seasons
A planet's **seasons** come from the tilt of its rotation axis, not its distance from the Sun. Earth
leans **23.5°**, so over a year each hemisphere takes turns tipping toward the Sun (summer) and away
(winter). Different worlds lean differently — Uranus is tipped nearly on its side. *Teaches: axial tilt
causes seasons.*

### FLARE! — the Sun
The Sun is a churning ball of plasma laced with magnetic fields. When those fields snap and reconnect,
they release **solar flares** and **coronal mass ejections** — bursts of radiation and plasma. The
largest reach Earth, where they spark **auroras** and can disturb satellites and power grids. *Teaches:
the Sun is active and ejects energy into the solar system.*

## Takeaways a player leaves with
- The solar system is a zoo of distinct body types, each with its own physics.
- Asteroids vs comets vs dwarf planets are **different things**, not synonyms.
- Seasons come from tilt; gas giants spin fast and bulge; the Moon needs rockets to land; the Sun is
  alive with flares; comet tails point away from the Sun.

## Sources to keep it honest
General planetary-science consensus (NASA Solar System Exploration; IAU dwarf-planet definition, 2006).
Facts are kept to robust, non-controversial statements; if a fact is ever sharpened, update the
microgame's `fact` string and this file together.
