# EDUCATION.md — Stellar Evolution

> The E in GAMES. What a player actually learns by playing — and the science behind each mechanic.

## The headline
**A star's mass at birth determines how it dies.** Everything else about a star — how long it
lives, how hot and bright it gets, how it ends — follows from that one number. The game is built so
the player *feels* this: every star shares a birth, then the **mass meter** forks the path.

## The life cycle (what the phases teach)

1. **Stellar Nursery (nebula).** Stars form when a giant cloud of gas and dust (mostly hydrogen)
   collapses under its own gravity. As it collapses it heats up. The more gas a clump pulls in
   (**accretion**), the more massive the star it becomes — which is why mashing here adds mass.

2. **Protostar.** The contracting core keeps heating until it crosses the ignition temperature
   (~10 million K) and **hydrogen fusion** switches on. That ignition is the moment a protostar
   becomes a true star — the timed tap is "catching" that ignition.

3. **Main Sequence.** A star spends ~90% of its life here, in **hydrostatic equilibrium**:
   gravity pulling inward is exactly balanced by the outward pressure of fusion. The balance mini-
   game is literally this tug-of-war — let gravity win and the star collapses; over-push and it
   swells. Massive stars burn hotter and bluer but live far *shorter* lives; small red stars sip
   their fuel for hundreds of billions of years. (The Sun: ~10 billion years, about halfway done.)

4. **Giant / Supergiant.** When core hydrogen runs out, the core contracts and the outer layers
   swell enormously and cool to red. Sun-like stars become **red giants**; massive stars become
   **red supergiants** (e.g., Betelgeuse).

### The fork — mass decides destiny

- **Low mass (≲ 8 M☉) → Planetary Nebula → White Dwarf.** The star gently puffs off its outer
  layers as a glowing **planetary nebula**, leaving behind a hot, Earth-sized **white dwarf** that
  slowly cools forever. This is the Sun's future.
- **High mass (≳ 8 M☉) → Supernova → Neutron Star or Black Hole.** The core fuses heavier and
  heavier elements until it builds iron, which cannot release fusion energy. The core collapses in
  under a second and rebounds as a **supernova** — briefly outshining a whole galaxy and forging and
  scattering heavy elements. What's left of the core becomes:
  - a **neutron star** (~8–20 M☉ progenitor): a city-sized ball so dense a teaspoon weighs billions
    of tons; fast-spinning ones are **pulsars**, sweeping beams like a lighthouse.
  - a **black hole** (≳ 20 M☉ progenitor): gravity so strong not even light escapes; matter spiraling
    in forms a glowing **accretion disk**.

## The two big takeaways
1. **Mass = destiny.** Sun-like star → white dwarf. Massive star → supernova → neutron star/black hole.
2. **A star is a balance.** It lives by holding gravity (inward) against fusion pressure (outward),
   and it dies when that balance finally breaks.

## The potato angle
Every supernova scatters the elements fusion forged — carbon, oxygen, iron, potassium. The
potassium in a potato, the iron in your blood, the carbon in everything alive: all of it was cooked
inside stars and flung out by the very supernovae this game lets you trigger. "You are made of star
stuff" is not a metaphor — and so is a potato.
