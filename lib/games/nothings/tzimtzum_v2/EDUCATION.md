# EDUCATION.md — Tzimtzum v2 (nothings)

> The educational spine of Tzimtzum v2. The game IS the lesson: to make *space* you must withdraw at a
> **measured, constant rate** — withdraw too violently and you collapse, too timidly and no space opens.
> The mechanic (drag the light's edge inward, keeping it on a constant-rate guide ring) is the concept made
> physical. This is the UX-pass alternative to `tzimtzum`; the lesson is preserved exactly, the input and
> pacing are made humane.

- **Scale (cell):** nothings — the pre-matter scale, where "something from nothing" is staged. Tzimtzum is
  the metaphysical companion to the Big Bang already on this scale: not an explosion *into* space, but the
  **making of space itself** by withdrawal. The most "nothing" act there is — the deliberate creation of
  emptiness so that anything else can exist.
- **Core concept:** **tzimtzum** (צמצום, "contraction" / "withdrawal") is the idea, from Lurianic
  Kabbalah, that an all-filling Infinite Light (*Ein Sof*) **contracted itself** to leave an empty space
  (the *chalal*, "void") in which a finite world could be created. Creation begins not with addition but
  with a self-limiting withdrawal.

---

## The big idea: making nothing is an act of restraint

If something fills *everything*, there is no room for anything else. Before a world can be made, room must
be made. Tzimtzum says the first creative act is **subtraction** — the Infinite voluntarily withdraws,
opening a bounded emptiness. Every later act of creation happens **inside that emptiness**.

The game encodes the part that is easy to miss in the telling: the withdrawal is **measured**. It is not a
violent collapse and not a hesitant flinch — it is a **constant, controlled rate** of contraction. That is
why the mechanic scores *alignment with a constant-rate guide*, not speed or distance:

- **Too fast — "you collapsed too hard."** Drag ahead of the guide ring and the alignment craters. A
  sudden, total withdrawal isn't creation; it's catastrophe. In Kabbalah this is the register of
  *shevirat ha-kelim*, the "shattering of the vessels" — when the withdrawal is too violent for the forms
  to hold, they break. The game flashes **TOO FAST**.
- **Too slow — "you barely contracted."** Lag behind the guide and you make no real room. A withdrawal too
  timid to open space leaves nothing able to come to be. The game flashes **TOO TIMID**.
- **Constant — ideal.** Stay on the ring as it contracts evenly and the void opens clean and stable.
  Maximum points; the rim glows green; a perfect final vessel reads **CREATION**.

---

## Why a *constant rate* is the right verb for this idea

Almost every spiritual or cosmological "making" is told as a single event — a word, a flash, a bang. But
the distinctive claim of tzimtzum is that creation-by-withdrawal is **sustained and measured**: the light
holds back, evenly, to keep the space open. A constant rate is the difference between *carving* space and
*detonating* it. By asking the player to physically trace a steady inward contraction for several seconds —
and by making both haste and hesitation fail — the game teaches the one thing a static picture of "the
void" can't: that the emptiness is **actively, evenly maintained**, not merely emptied once.

This also mirrors a very ordinary truth the player feels in their hand: steady control is harder than a
quick jerk. Keeping your edge exactly on a contracting ring is genuinely difficult — which is exactly the
point. Restraint is work. (v2 makes the *input* humane — one finger or a mouse, a single visible ring to
trace — without softening the *demand* for evenness.)

## On-canvas: the lesson you can see

- A field of **light** fills the screen (the Ein Sof), with a glowing edge you control.
- As you drag inward the light **withdraws** and a dark **void (chalal)** opens around it; as you drag out
  the light **floods back**. The grab knob on the edge says "pull me in".
- The dashed **guide ring** makes the central demand literal: keep your edge **on the ring** as it
  contracts at a constant rate — neither inside it (a collapse) nor outside it (a stall). The whole
  instrument is one ring, so the lesson reads instantly.

---

## Why this is the right lesson for "nothings"

The Nothings scale is about what precedes matter. The Big Bang answers "how did *stuff* begin"; tzimtzum
answers the stranger prior question — "how did *room for stuff* begin?" The answer it offers is
counter-intuitive and worth feeling in the body: **nothing** had to be *made*, deliberately, by something
that drew itself back at a careful, constant rate. Emptiness as an achievement, not a default.

## Potato angle

Russ would tell you the hardest part of building anything — a company, a game, a roster — is making the
empty space for it: saying no, withdrawing, leaving room. Pull in too hard and you collapse the whole
thing; don't pull at all and nothing new can fit. Uhhh... maybe creation really is just knowing exactly how
much to hold back, and keeping that hand steady. Tzimtzum v2 is the game that makes a player practice the
restraint — with one finger, on the ring, all the way down.
