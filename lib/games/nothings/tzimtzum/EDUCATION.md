# EDUCATION.md — Tzimtzum (nothings)

> The educational spine of Tzimtzum. The game IS the lesson: to make *space* you must withdraw at a
> **measured, constant rate** — withdraw too violently and you collapse, too timidly and no space opens.
> The mechanic (hold a constant rate of two-finger contraction) is the concept made physical.

- **Scale (cell):** nothings — the pre-matter scale, where "something from nothing" is staged. Tzimtzum is
  the metaphysical companion to the Big Bang already on this scale: not an explosion *into* space, but the
  **making of space itself** by withdrawal. The most "nothing" act there is — the deliberate creation of
  emptiness so that anything else can exist.
- **Core concept:** **tzimtzum** (צמצום, "contraction" / "withdrawal") is the idea, from Lurianic
  Kabbalah, that an all-filling Infinite Light (*Ein Sof*) **contracted itself** to leave an empty space
  (the *chalal*, "void") in which a finite world could be created. Creation begins not with addition but
  with a self-limiting withdrawal.

---

## The big idea: making nothing is an act of restraint

If something fills *everything*, there is no room for anything else. Before a world can be made, room must
be made. Tzimtzum says the first creative act is **subtraction** — the Infinite voluntarily withdraws,
opening a bounded emptiness. Every later act of creation happens **inside that emptiness**.

The game encodes the part that is easy to miss in the telling: the withdrawal is **measured**. It is not a
violent collapse and not a hesitant flinch — it is a **constant, controlled rate** of contraction. That is
why the mechanic scores *steadiness of rate*, not speed or distance:

- **Too fast — "you collapsed too hard."** A sudden, total withdrawal isn't creation; it's catastrophe.
  In Kabbalah this is the register of *shevirat ha-kelim*, the "shattering of the vessels" — when the
  inflow/withdrawal is too violent for the forms to hold, they break. The game punishes the snap-pinch.
- **Too slow — "you barely contracted."** A withdrawal too timid to open real space makes no room; nothing
  can come to be. The game gives few points for a hold that never commits to a rate.
- **Constant — ideal.** A smooth, deliberate, even contraction opens a clean, stable void. Maximum points.

---

## Why a *constant rate* is the right verb for this idea

Almost every spiritual or cosmological "making" is told as a single event — a word, a flash, a bang. But
the distinctive claim of tzimtzum is that creation-by-withdrawal is **sustained and measured**: the light
holds back, evenly, to keep the space open. A constant rate is the difference between *carving* space and
*detonating* it. By asking the player to physically hold a steady contraction for several seconds — and by
making both haste and hesitation fail — the game teaches the one thing a static picture of "the void"
can't: that the emptiness is **actively, evenly maintained**, not merely emptied once.

This also mirrors a very ordinary truth the player feels in their hands: steady control is harder than a
quick jerk. Keeping a pinch perfectly even for four seconds is genuinely difficult — which is exactly the
point. Restraint is work.

## On-canvas: the lesson you can see

- A field of **light** fills the screen (the Ein Sof).
- As you pinch, the light **withdraws** and a dark **void (chalal)** opens at the centre, its rim glowing
  where withdrawal meets light. As you stretch, the light **floods back** and the space closes.
- The **rate gauge** makes the central demand literal: keep the marker in the band — neither a collapse
  (right of the band) nor a stall (left of it).

---

## Why this is the right lesson for "nothings"

The Nothings scale is about what precedes matter. The Big Bang answers "how did *stuff* begin"; tzimtzum
answers the stranger prior question — "how did *room for stuff* begin?" The answer it offers is
counter-intuitive and worth feeling in the body: **nothing** had to be *made*, deliberately, by something
that drew itself back at a careful, constant rate. Emptiness as an achievement, not a default.

## Potato angle

Russ would tell you the hardest part of building anything — a company, a game, a roster — is making the
empty space for it: saying no, withdrawing, leaving room. Pinch too hard and you collapse the whole
thing; don't pinch at all and nothing new can fit. Uhhh... maybe creation really is just knowing exactly
how much to hold back. Tzimtzum is the game that makes a player practice the restraint.
