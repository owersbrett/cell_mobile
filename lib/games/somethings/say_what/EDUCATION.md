# Say What? — Education (E)

**Subject:** phonology and English orthography — the gap between how words are
**spelled** and how they **sound**, plus homophones, elision, and the play
language of pig latin.

## What the player learns (by doing, not reading)
The teaching is the mechanic. To score, the player must convert a written string
into **sound** and match that sound to a known phrase — decoding by ear, not by
eye. Repeating this builds real phonological awareness:

1. **English spelling is not phonetic.** The same sound has many spellings
   (`GNU` / `New` / `knew`), and the same letters make different sounds. Reading
   a fauxnetic string forces the player to trust the *sound*, not the letters —
   the core skill behind sounding out any unfamiliar word.
2. **Homophones carry meaning in speech.** `SEE FOOD` and `seafood`, `ICE CREAM`
   and `I scream` are acoustically identical — only context disambiguates them.
   The game makes the ambiguity the puzzle, which is exactly why puns work.
3. **Word boundaries live in the sound, not the space.** `AISLE BEE BACK` has to
   be re-segmented into `I'll be back`; `EGG SAMPLE` collapses into `example`.
   Spoken language runs words together (elision / liaison); the reader's brain
   re-inserts the boundaries. That re-segmentation is the whole game.
4. **Pig latin is rule-based sound transformation.** `ELLO-HAY` = "hello" with
   the leading consonant cluster moved to the end + "ay". Decoding it teaches
   that a made-up language can be perfectly regular — an on-ramp to how phonetic
   rules and morphology work.
5. **Fluent readers subvocalize.** The winning strategy — say it out loud, or
   in your head — is literally the strategy skilled readers use on hard words.
   The game rewards the correct reading process.

## Vocabulary surfaced in play
homophone · fauxnetic / phonetic respelling · elision · word boundary /
segmentation · subvocalization · pig latin · pun · orthography vs. phonology.

## Standards touchpoints
- CCSS **RF (Reading: Foundational Skills)** — phonics and word recognition:
  decoding, phoneme–grapheme correspondence, and reading irregularly spelled
  words by sound.
- CCSS **L.5.5** — figurative language and word relationships (puns / plays on
  homophones as intentional wordplay).
