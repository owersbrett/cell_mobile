# EDUCATION.md — Osmosis v2

> The educational component (the **E** in GAMES). The lesson is taught **inside the mechanic** —
> you don't read a fact card, you keep a potato cell alive and feel the biology do it. The lesson
> is **identical** to the original `osmosis`; v2 only sharpens how legibly it lands.

## Learning objective

After a round, a player can explain:
1. **Osmosis** — water diffuses across a semi-permeable membrane **toward the side with the higher
   solute concentration** (the cell can't pump water; it only responds to the balance outside).
2. The three tonicity states **relative to the cell**, and what each does:
   | Solution | Solute outside vs inside | Water moves | Cell |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Hypotonic** | lower outside | **into** the cell | swells → may **lyse (burst)** |
   | **Isotonic** | equal | no net movement | stable **turgor** (firm) |
   | **Hypertonic** | higher outside | **out of** the cell | shrinks → **crenates (shrivels)** |
3. **Turgor** — the firm, water-filled state of a healthy cell (a firm fry vs a limp one), and why
   cells live in a narrow safe range, not at any volume.
4. **Why cells burst or shrivel** — it isn't the salt itself; it's *water following the salt*. Too
   dilute outside and the cell over-fills and ruptures; too salty and it dries out and crumples.

## How the mechanic teaches it (no fact cards)

- **The knob track IS the tonicity vocabulary.** A blue→green→amber deck labelled WATER /
  ISOTONIC / SALT. The knob's position is the solution's tonicity — the player *operates* the
  vocabulary as the control surface, not a footnote, and the green centre is literally the
  isotonic target.
- **Cause→effect is one frame, one value.** Where you put the knob *is* what the cell feels, so the
  flux arrows (inward when hypotonic, outward when hypertonic, gone at isotonic) and the cell's
  swell/shrivel respond instantly. There's no abstract intermediate to decode — drag toward water
  and watch it fill; drag toward salt and watch it crumple.
- **The cell itself responds:** it **swells** (taut, paling rim, strain reddening) toward lysis, or
  **crenates** (spiky inward dimples buckling the membrane) toward shrivel. The caption names the
  state — "SWELLING — bursting (lysis)", "SHRIVELING — crenation", "FIRM TURGOR" — and the
  sub-caption gives the cause ("hypertonic — water leaving cell →").
- **The fail events ARE the definitions:** over-filling triggers **"LYSED — too much water!"**;
  drying out triggers **"CRENATED — too salty!"**. The player earns the term by causing the event.
- **The control encodes the chemistry:** dragging toward **WATER** dilutes the solution (hypotonic,
  swells the cell); toward **SALT** concentrates it (hypertonic, shrivels it). Holding the cell
  healthy means keeping the *outside* matched to the *inside* — finding isotonic — which is exactly
  the biological condition for stable turgor.

## Misconceptions corrected

- *"Salt kills cells directly."* No — a hypertonic solution pulls **water** out; the cell dies of
  dehydration, and the same salt at isotonic concentration is harmless.
- *"Pure water is always safest."* No — a hypotonic (too-dilute) environment over-fills the cell and
  bursts it. This is why IV fluids are isotonic.
- *"The cell pumps water to defend itself."* In this model it can't — osmosis is passive; the only
  lever is the solute balance outside, which is exactly what the player drags.

## Scale fit

Sits on **`BioScale.cell`** alongside Hungry Cell, Mitosis Rush and Meiosis. Where those cover
survival, division and inheritance, Osmosis covers **membrane transport and homeostasis** — the
moment-to-moment water balance every living cell must hold. The potato-cell skin foreshadows the
plant-cell extension (turgor, plasmolysis) without changing the animal-cell lysis lesson.
