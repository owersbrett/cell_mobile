# Meiosis — How to Play

> Player-facing. Anyone reading this should understand the game. This is one entry in the
> Manual and a source file for the deployable Manual app. Plain language, no code.
> **STATUS: Spec only — game is not yet built. This manual describes the intended experience.**

**Scale:** Cell · **Type:** Solo (chase the high score) · **Length:** ~90 s (8 phases across 2 divisions)

---

## The goal

One cell needs to become **four**. And not just any four — four genetically unique cells (gametes)
that could each become a seed. You'll guide the cell through two rounds of division, with a gene-swapping
challenge at the start that makes every run different.

---

## What makes this different from Mitosis Rush

Mitosis Rush ends in 2 identical cells. Meiosis ends in **4 unique cells**, and the path is
longer: two consecutive divisions, with no DNA copying between them. The big challenge — the
one that doesn't exist in Mitosis Rush — is **crossing over**, where you swap gene segments
between chromosome pairs to create variety.

---

## Division I — The Reduction Division

### Phase 1 — Prophase I: Crossing Over (15 s)
This is the heart of meiosis. Chromosome pairs from each parent line up side-by-side. You
need to **swap one gene segment** between each pair — drag a colored band from one chromosome
and drop it onto the matching position on its partner. You'll see a spark and a small X mark
(a chiasma) where the swap happened. Swap all 4 pairs to complete the phase.

Why does this matter? Because every swap makes the resulting gametes slightly different from
either parent. **This is how genetic variation works.**

### Phase 2 — Metaphase I (10 s)
The paired chromosomes need to line up in the center of the cell. **Drag each bivalent pair**
to the glowing center line until it snaps. Note: you're aligning whole pairs here, not individual
chromosomes. 4 bivalents to place.

### Phase 3 — Anaphase I (12 s)
**Swipe each pair** up or down to pull the homologous chromosomes apart to opposite poles.
Unlike in Mitosis Rush, the chromosomes don't split here — you're pulling **whole chromosomes**
(each still has two chromatids joined). 4 pairs to swipe.

### Phase 4 — Telophase I + Cytokinesis I (8 s)
Two new cells form, each with half the original chromosomes. **Tap each nucleus** 3 times to
seal it, then **drag the furrow** to split the cell. You'll see both cells are labeled "n" —
haploid — half the DNA of the original.

---

## Division II — The Separation Division

> *"NO DNA REPLICATION — Division II begins now."*
>
> This matters: unlike between divisions in normal growth, there is no copying phase here.

### Phase 5 — Metaphase II (8 s)
Both cells are shown side-by-side. In each cell, **drag the chromosomes** to the center plate.
This time you're working in both cells simultaneously — 2 chromosomes per cell, 4 total.

### Phase 6 — Anaphase II (12 s)
Now the sister chromatids finally separate — just like in Mitosis Rush. **Swipe each pair**
up or down in both cells. These chromatids are thinner and shorter than the ones in Anaphase I,
which is how you can tell the difference.

### Phase 7 — Telophase II + Cytokinesis II (8 s)
Four nucleus outlines appear across both cells. **Tap each one** 3 times to seal it, then drag
to cleave both cells. When it's done, **four cells appear** — each glowing with a unique color
pattern showing that their gene contents are different.

---

## How you score

- **Crossing Over (Prophase I):** +30 for each successful gene swap (4 pairs = 120 points max).
- **All other phases:** 50 base points for completing, plus a speed bonus up to 100 based on
  time remaining.
- **Maximum total:** around 1170 points.

The results screen shows all 8 phase scores. Tap to play again.

---

## Tips

- In **Prophase I**, position matters for the swap — match the band's position (top, middle,
  or bottom of the chromosome) as well as its color. A wrong drop just cancels; no penalty.
- In **Metaphase II**, both cells run at the same time. Prioritize one cell, then the other —
  don't try to alternate every drag.
- In **Anaphase II**, look at the thickness of the chromosomes to confirm you're in Division II
  (thinner = single chromatids). Same swipe gesture as always.

---

## What it teaches

Why meiosis produces four genetically different cells instead of identical copies — and why
that difference is what makes seeds, breeding, and genetic diversity possible. The crossing-over
swap in Phase 1 is the specific moment where new combinations are created.
