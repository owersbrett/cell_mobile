# EDUCATION.md — Transcribe v2

> The educational component (the **E** in GAMES). The lesson is taught **inside the mechanic** — you
> don't read a fact card to win, you literally run transcription then translation. The lesson is
> **identical** to the original `transcribe`; v2 only sharpens how legibly and fairly it lands.

## Learning objective

After a round, a player can:

1. **Transcribe** a DNA template strand into mRNA, applying the base-pairing rule **with uracil**:

   | DNA template | mRNA base |
   |---|---|
   | A | **U** (not T — mRNA uses uracil) |
   | T | A |
   | C | G |
   | G | C |

2. **Translate** an mRNA codon (3 bases) into an amino acid using the **standard genetic code** — and
   recognise that **Stop codons** (UAA, UAG, UGA) terminate the protein.

3. Narrate the **central dogma**: **DNA → mRNA → protein**, performed by **RNA polymerase** (transcription)
   then the **ribosome** (translation) — the two roles the player's thumb plays in sequence.

## How the mechanic teaches it (no quiz screen)

- **You operate the enzyme.** Transcribing is the literal action: read the template base, tap the
  complement. The **U-replaces-T curveball** is front-and-centre — the legend says it, the buttons offer
  **U** where you'd expect T, and the complement-tinted ghost ring trains the pairing by colour.
- **Three bases visibly become one amino acid.** The codon-progress dots fill as you transcribe; on the
  third clean base the molecule snaps into a codon and you switch from polymerase to **ribosome** to
  name the amino acid. The pipeline-as-tapping-loop IS the lesson — you feel why a codon is 3 bases.
- **The first two codons auto-reveal the code** (`AUG → Met`), teaching the mapping before it's quizzed.
  From the third on, you recall it yourself from three choices — easy to learn, then harder to master.
- **Stop codons are real biology, not a gimmick.** When a codon is UAA/UAG/UGA the choice is `STOP`;
  picking it correctly is recognising **translation termination** — the protein is released.
- **The protein is assembled in front of you.** Each amino you (or the ribosome) names is appended to a
  growing polypeptide strip, and the round ends on a **PROTEIN ASSEMBLED** reveal — so the abstract
  "gene codes for a protein" becomes a thing you watched yourself build, base by base.
- **A broken base breaks the protein.** A wrong/late transcription marks the codon dirty, so it makes no
  amino acid — modelling how a transcription error corrupts the downstream product.

## Misconceptions corrected

- *"mRNA has thymine like DNA."* No — **mRNA uses uracil (U) in place of thymine (T)**. This is the
  single most-tested teaching point and the game's core curveball.
- *"A codon is one base."* No — a codon is **three** consecutive mRNA bases; the dots and the
  every-third-base translation make the triplet tangible.
- *"Every codon is an amino acid."* No — **Stop codons code for no amino acid**; they end the protein.
- *"Transcription and translation are the same step."* No — transcription (DNA→mRNA, polymerase) precedes
  translation (mRNA→protein, ribosome); the game makes you switch roles between them.

## Scale fit

Sits on **`BioScale.cell`** alongside Osmosis, Hungry Cell, Mitosis Rush, Meiosis and Cell Type. Where
those cover homeostasis, survival, division and classification, **Transcribe covers gene expression** —
how the information in DNA becomes the working molecules of the cell, the central dogma every later
topic depends on.

## Extensions (future)

- A **promoter / start-codon** find step before transcription begins (where does the gene start?).
- A **tRNA anticodon** variant — match the anticodon instead of recalling the amino directly.
- **Point-mutation** rounds: a single swapped base that does or doesn't change the amino (silent vs
  missense vs nonsense).
