# EDUCATION.md — Cell Type

> The E in GAMES. The educational component, and how it is delivered **inside the mechanic** —
> not as a wall of text the player skips.

## Learning objective

A player who runs a few sessions can look at an unlabeled cell and reason about its kingdom from
its structures: **plant, animal, bacterial, or fungal.** They internalize the diagnostic
differences between the four cell types — the standard cell-biology comparison that appears in
every introductory course.

## The core concept being taught

**Cells differ by the structures they carry, and those differences map to how the organism lives.**

| Structure | Plant | Animal | Bacterial | Fungal | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | yes (cellulose) | **no** | yes (peptidoglycan) + capsule | yes (chitin) | rigidity, protection, shape |
| Chloroplasts | **yes** | no | no | no | photosynthesis — makes its own food |
| Large central vacuole | **yes** | no (small vesicles) | no | small only | water storage, turgor pressure |
| True nucleus | yes | yes | **no — nucleoid** | yes | eukaryote vs prokaryote divide |
| Relative size | large | large | **~10x smaller** | medium | prokaryotes are tiny |
| Motility tail | — | — | flagellum (often) | — | swimming |

## Education delivered IN the mechanic (not bolted on)

1. **The drawing is the lesson.** Every structure listed above is *visibly rendered* — the player
   must read the rigid box wall, the green grana-striped chloroplasts, the giant pale vacuole, the
   central nucleus vs the free nucleoid tangle, the tiny size, the flagellum. Classifying correctly
   *is* recognizing the structure. There is no separate "study" step.
2. **The fact card closes the loop.** After every answer a structural fact for the true type is
   shown (e.g. "Fungal cells have a wall of chitin but NO chloroplasts — fungi never
   photosynthesise"). Right or wrong, the player gets the *reason* the cell was what it was.
3. **The difficulty ramp teaches the hierarchy of tells.** Early cells shout (many chloroplasts,
   obvious rod + flagellum). Late cells go quiet, forcing the player onto the *decisive* tells —
   "is there a nucleus?", "is there a wall?" — which is exactly the diagnostic order a biologist
   uses. Subtlety isn't just harder; it trains better discrimination.
4. **The four-way contrast is the point.** PLANT vs ANIMAL teaches wall + chloroplasts + vacuole.
   FUNGAL is deliberately the in-between (walled like a plant, heterotroph like an animal) so the
   player can't shortcut "wall = plant". BACTERIAL teaches the prokaryote/eukaryote divide via the
   missing nucleus and the dramatic size difference.

## Misconceptions actively corrected

- "All cells have a nucleus" → bacteria don't (nucleoid). Corrected every time a bacterial cell
  appears and the fact card fires.
- "A wall means it's a plant" → fungi (and bacteria) have walls too; the FUNGAL option forces the
  distinction onto chloroplasts and shape.
- "Bigger detail = bigger importance" → the single most decisive tell (size for bacteria, wall
  for animals) is often the simplest, not the busiest.

## Scope honesty

This game teaches the **four-kingdom structural comparison** at an introductory level. It does not
cover organelle-level function depth (ER, Golgi, lysosomes), archaea as a separate domain, or
algae/protists. Those belong to other cell-scale games (e.g. Hungry Cell's organelle pickups,
Mitosis Rush's division mechanics). Cell Type owns the *classification* lesson cleanly.
