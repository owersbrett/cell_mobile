# Tissue Type v2 — EDUCATION.md (the E in GAMES)

The education is **in the mechanic**: to score you must visually distinguish the
four primary tissue types by morphology, and every answer surfaces a fact about
the exact subtype you just saw. v2 keeps the lesson fully intact and makes it
*stickier* by removing the clock-stop — facts now stream in a non-blocking ticker
so repetition under time pressure builds the pattern-recognition a histology
student drills, without breaking the rhythm.

## The four primary tissue types

### 1. Epithelial — sheets & linings
Cells packed **edge-to-edge** with almost no space between them, forming
continuous sheets that cover surfaces and line cavities/glands. Always sits on a
basement membrane. Functions: protection, absorption, secretion, filtration.
- **Squamous** — flat tile-like cells (air sacs, vessel linings; thin for gas exchange).
- **Cuboidal** — cube cells (kidney tubules, glands; secretion/absorption).
- **Columnar** — tall cells, often with microvilli (gut lining; absorption).
- **Stratified** — stacked layers (skin surface; takes abrasion).

### 2. Connective — cells scattered in a matrix
**Few cells far apart** in a large extracellular matrix — the *space between
cells* is the defining feature. Most abundant/diverse tissue. Functions: support,
binding, storage, transport, defence.
- **Adipose (fat)** — cells ballooned by one oil droplet, nucleus at the rim.
- **Bone** — cells in rings (lacunae) around a central canal; hard mineral matrix.
- **Blood** — a FLUID matrix (plasma) carrying red and white cells.
- **Areolar** — a loose fibre web with scattered cells; packs around organs.

### 3. Muscle — parallel fibres that contract
Long fibres aligned in **parallel**, packed with contractile proteins.
- **Skeletal** — long striated (cross-striped) fibres, peripheral nuclei; voluntary.
- **Cardiac** — branching striated fibres joined by intercalated discs; involuntary, tireless.
- **Smooth** — tapered spindle cells, no striations, one central nucleus; involuntary (gut, vessels).

### 4. Nervous — neurons + glia
**Star-shaped cell bodies** trailing branching processes that wire the body for
communication.
- **Neuron** — cell body with many dendrites (receive) and one long axon (send).
- **Glia** — smaller support cells that vastly outnumber neurons; feed and insulate them.

## Reading tells (the visual rubric — echoed in the on-screen ticker)
| See this… | Think… |
|---|---|
| Cells crammed into a continuous sheet | Epithelial |
| Lots of empty matrix, cells far apart | Connective |
| Long parallel fibres (often striped) | Muscle |
| Star cells with long branches | Nervous |

The neutral ticker prompt — **"sheet · scatter · fibre · star"** — keeps that
four-way rubric in front of newcomers between answers, a light scaffold that costs
no extra mechanic.

## Why H&E colours are uniform
Real slides are stained with the same dyes regardless of tissue, so colour can't
classify them — only structure can. The game keeps the stain uniform for exactly
this reason: it trains the eye on shape, the skill that actually transfers.
