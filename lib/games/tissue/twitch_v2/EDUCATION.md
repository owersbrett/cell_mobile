# Twitch v2 — EDUCATION.md (the **E** in GAMES)

**Scale:** Tissue (skeletal muscle) · learning is *in the mechanic*, not a quiz.

> v2 teaches the same reflex-arc + refuel lesson as base Twitch (see
> `twitch/EDUCATION.md`). It is tuned tighter — faster escalation, quicker protein
> cadence — but the concepts are identical.

## The big idea
A muscle twitch is a **reflex arc**: a motor neuron fires an **impulse** (action
potential) down the **axon** to the **neuromuscular junction**, which triggers the
muscle to contract. The loop — watch the impulse leave the source, tap the target the
instant it lands — is that cause-and-effect made playable.

## Concept 1 — Excitation: the nerve drives the muscle
A muscle never contracts on its own; it **waits for the signal**. The **SOURCE** is
the motor-neuron soma, the impulse races down the axon, and the **TARGET** is the
junction on the muscle. You react to the arrival — **timing**, not mashing, is the
skill. Since a real neuron never fires from one fixed place, the source and target
**move every reflex**: react to *where* and *when*, don't memorize a rhythm.

## Concept 2 — Reaction time gets tested, then pushed
A reflex has a real latency (stimulus → response). The game makes that the tightening
strike window: every clean reflex the impulse travels faster and the window narrows,
so your effective reaction time must improve — the way trained, repeated firing
sharpens a motor response, up to a hard limit (the ramp passes human reach by design).

## Concept 3 — Fuel: muscles run on protein
Repeated contraction spends the muscle's resources; it has to **refuel and repair**,
and the raw material for muscle is **protein** (amino acids build actin/myosin and
repair micro-damage from use). Every eight clean reflexes the game stops for **NOW EAT
PROTEIN!** — rapid-tap the dish spread to gather fuel before the muscle can keep
firing. It's the health-season message in the mechanic: work the muscle, then feed it.

## Takeaway
Impulse leaves the neuron → travels the axon → lands at the junction → the muscle
twitches; reaction time is the trainable skill; and a working muscle must be
**refuelled with protein**. Players feel the reflex-and-recover cycle rather than
memorizing it.
