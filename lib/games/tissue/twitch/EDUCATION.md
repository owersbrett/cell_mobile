# Twitch — EDUCATION.md (the **E** in GAMES)

**Scale:** Tissue (skeletal muscle) · learning is *in the mechanic*, not a quiz bolted on.

## The big idea
A muscle twitch is a **reflex arc**: a motor neuron fires an **impulse** (action
potential) that travels down the **axon** and reaches the **neuromuscular junction**,
which triggers the muscle to contract. The game's core loop — watch the impulse leave
the source, tap the target the instant it lands — is that cause-and-effect made
playable, and the fuel a muscle needs to keep firing is made playable too.

## Concept 1 — Excitation: the nerve drives the muscle
A muscle does not contract on its own; it **waits for the signal**. In Twitch the
**SOURCE** is the motor-neuron soma, the impulse races down the axon, and the
**TARGET** is the neuromuscular junction on the muscle. You react to the arrival —
which is why **timing**, not mashing, is the skill. Because a real neuron never fires
from exactly one place, the source and target **move every reflex**: the skill is
reacting to *where* and *when*, not memorizing a rhythm.

## Concept 2 — Reaction time gets tested, then pushed
A reflex has a real latency — the time from stimulus to response. The game turns that
into the tightening **strike window**: every clean reflex the impulse travels faster
and the window narrows, so your effective reaction time has to improve. This mirrors
how trained, repeated firing sharpens a motor response — and how, past a limit, it
becomes impossible (the difficulty ramps past human reach by design).

## Concept 3 — Fuel: muscles run on protein
Repeated contraction spends the muscle's resources; it has to **refuel and repair**,
and the raw material for muscle is **protein** (amino acids build the actin and myosin
filaments and repair micro-damage from use). Every ten clean reflexes the game stops
for **NOW EAT PROTEIN!** — a spread of dishes you rapid-tap to gather fuel before the
muscle can keep firing. It's the health-season message in the mechanic: work the
muscle, then feed it.

## Takeaway
Impulse leaves the neuron → travels the axon → lands at the junction → the muscle
twitches; reaction time is the skill and it's trainable to a limit; and a working
muscle must be **refuelled with protein**. Players feel the reflex-and-recover cycle
rather than memorizing it.
