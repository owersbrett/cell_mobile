# EDUCATION.md — Body Map

> The educational component (the **E** in GAMES). The learning is **in the mechanic**: to score,
> you must place each organ where it actually sits in the body. Repetition under a shrinking timer
> and tightening zones turns "where is the liver?" from a guess into recall.

## Learning objective
By the end of a run a player can locate the major human organs by region:
**which organ lives in the head, the chest, the upper abdomen, the flanks, the lower abdomen,
and the pelvis** — and roughly left vs. right.

## What each placement teaches

| Organ | Where it sits | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| **Brain** | Inside the skull (head) | Protected by bone; runs the whole body. |
| **Heart** | Center of the chest, tilted slightly to the left | Pumps blood; sits between the lungs. |
| **Lungs** | Flanking the heart, filling both sides of the chest | Take in air; the heart nestles between them. |
| **Liver** | Upper-right abdomen, under the ribs | The body's largest internal organ; filters blood, makes bile. |
| **Stomach** | Upper-left abdomen | Receives food from the esophagus; starts digestion. |
| **Spleen** | Upper-left, behind the stomach | Filters blood and helps the immune system. |
| **Pancreas** | Behind the stomach, center | Makes digestive juices and insulin. |
| **Kidneys** | Mid-back, one on each side of the spine | Filter waste from blood into urine. |
| **Intestines** | Coiled through the lower abdomen | Absorb nutrients (small) and water (large). |
| **Bladder** | Low in the pelvis | Stores urine until it leaves the body. |

## How the game reinforces it
- **Ghost ring (rounds 1–2):** the correct spot pulses faintly while you learn — scaffolding.
- **Ghost removed (round 3+):** the scaffolding disappears; you must remember the position.
- **Tightening zones:** as you improve, the acceptable drop area shrinks, demanding precision.
- **Accuracy bonus:** dropping near the true center pays more than a sloppy hit — rewarding
  a sharper mental map.
- **Landmark-first order:** big organs (brain, heart, lungs) come first; small, easily-confused
  ones (spleen, pancreas, kidneys) join later, once the landmarks anchor the layout.

## Potato tie-in (cross-scale)
The companion **Organ Rush** quiz alternates human organs with plant/potato organs (root, tuber,
stem). Body Map focuses on the human body so the spatial map is unambiguous — but the broader
"organs are specialized parts with a place and a job" idea carries straight across to the potato.

## Honest scope
These are coarse, kid-friendly landmarks, not a surgical atlas. Left/right labels follow the
player's on-screen view (a simplification). The aim is a durable mental picture of *where things
are*, which is exactly the gap a naming quiz leaves open.
