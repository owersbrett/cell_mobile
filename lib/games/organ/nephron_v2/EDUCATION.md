# Nephron v2 — EDUCATION.md

**Subject:** kidney filtration — the nephron. **Audience:** middle-school and up.

## The single load-bearing idea (it IS the mechanic)
The nephron tubule's **default is excretion**: a molecule you don't act on leaves
in the **urine**. **Reabsorption is the active, selective step** — you must reach
in and pull a needed molecule back into the **blood**. In the game, doing nothing
sends a molecule to urine; that's *correct for waste* and a *loss for a nutrient*.
That asymmetry is the whole kidney in one rule.

## What the player learns by playing
- **Reabsorb → blood:** glucose, water (H₂O), amino acids, and the ions the body
  needs (Na⁺, K⁺, HCO₃⁻). These are recovered from the filtrate so they aren't
  wasted.
- **Excrete → urine:** metabolic waste — urea, creatinine, uric acid, ammonia —
  plus toxins and drug metabolites the body must clear.
- **Regulation, not all-or-nothing:** the same substance can be kept *or* dumped
  depending on the body's needs. The top-of-ramp **Na⁺ vs Na⁺·EXCESS** call
  (identical orb, only the label differs) teaches that the kidney reabsorbs the
  *amount the body needs* and excretes the *surplus* — real homeostatic tuning of
  blood pressure and electrolyte balance.

## Why "blood purity" matters
Letting waste back into the blood (or losing nutrients) is what failing kidneys
do. The **BLOOD PURITY** gauge dramatizes the consequence of bad filtration —
but, unlike a real medical emergency, it never ends your turn; it just signals
tension and eases the flow so you can recover. The takeaway: clean blood depends
on the nephron getting these calls right, millions of times per day.

## Real-world hook
Each human kidney holds ~1 million nephrons filtering ~180 L of fluid daily, of
which ~99% is reabsorbed. Dialysis exists precisely because this selective
reabsorb/excrete step is so hard to replace.
