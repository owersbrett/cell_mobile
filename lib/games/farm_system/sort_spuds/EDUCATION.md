# EDUCATION.md — Sort the Spuds (Grading & Quality Control)

> The E in GAMES for the farm-system scale: what a player actually learns by playing. The mechanic
> *is* the lesson — you grade and cull because real graders grade and cull.

## The big idea
Between the field and the shelf, every potato passes a **grading line**. Grading sorts produce by
**size** and removes **defects** so that what reaches the buyer is uniform, safe, and priced fairly.
The game compresses that line into 60 seconds: a belt, four bins, and a stream of spuds to judge.

## 1. Why sort by size?
Buyers pay for **uniformity**. A bag of evenly-sized potatoes cooks evenly, packs cleanly, and meets a
spec a restaurant or supermarket ordered. Size also sets **price**: large bakers command more than
small "creamers," so separating them lets a grower sell each at its best value instead of dumping a
mixed lot at the lowest common price.
- **In-game:** SMALL / MEDIUM / LARGE bins. Mis-sorting a size costs points — a mixed pack is worth
  less than a graded one.

## 2. Why cull defects? (the costly mistake)
A single bad potato can ruin a whole crate. **Rot** spreads; a soft, infected tuber will infect its
neighbours in storage. So defective potatoes are **culled** — pulled off the line before they reach
sale — and diverted to feed, starch, or compost.
- **In-game:** a rotten spud sent to a SALE bin is the **biggest penalty (−30)** — it "contaminates
  the crate." Culling a defect correctly is the **best reward (+14)**, because catching the bad one is
  the whole point of quality control.

## 3. The three defects you're catching
- **Rotten** — dark, mushy patches from bacterial or fungal soft rot. Unsellable and contagious.
- **Green** — skin that has greened from light exposure. Green tissue carries **solanine**, a natural
  toxin that is bitter and, in quantity, unsafe. Greened potatoes are pulled from sale.
- **Blemished / scab** — scab spots, growth cracks, or damage. Not dangerous, but below table grade;
  downgraded to processing or feed.

## 4. Grade standards & the supply chain
Real systems formalise this. US grades (e.g. **U.S. No. 1**) demand potatoes that are firm, fairly
clean, fairly well-shaped, and free of serious defects, within a size range. Graders — human and
optical-sorting machines — make these calls fast, all day. The game's **accelerating belt** and
**subtler defects** mirror the real pressure: speed plus a sharp eye, sustained over a long shift.

## 5. The thread to the bigger picture
Quality control isn't one step — it repeats at harvest, washing, packing, and retail. Each pass
protects the **brand**: the buyer trusts that what's in the bag matches the label. The grading line is
where a farm's reputation is enforced, one spud at a time.

## Fact lines surfaced in-game
The rotating strip (refreshed on each clean cull) pulls from these:
- Graders sort by SIZE so buyers get a uniform pack.
- Cull the bad ones — one rotten spud spoils the whole crate.
- Green skin = solanine. Greened potatoes are pulled from sale.
- Blemishes & scab downgrade a potato below table grade.
- US grades: No.1 is clean & well-shaped; culls go to feed or starch.
- Sorting by size sets the price — big bakers earn more than smalls.
- Quality control protects the brand at every step of the chain.
