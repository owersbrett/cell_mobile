# EDUCATION.md — Pollination Dash (the E in GAMES)

> The educational component. In Pollination Dash the lesson is not a popup — it **is the win
> condition**: you only score by moving pollen between two different flowers of the same species,
> which is exactly how pollination sets fruit and seed.

## Core concept: what pollination is
**Pollination is the transfer of pollen** from the male part of a flower (the **anther**) to the
female part (the **stigma**). When pollen reaches a compatible stigma — usually on **another flower
of the same species** — it fertilizes the ovule, and the flower can **set seed and fruit**. Without
that transfer, most flowering plants make no fruit.

In the game:
- **Loading pollen** at a flower = the bee picking pollen up on its body (as real bees do — pollen
  sticks to their fuzzy hairs).
- **Pollinating a second same-species flower** = depositing that pollen on a receptive flower so it
  sets fruit. The little **potato** that appears is the "fruit set."
- The **"must visit a different flower" rule** models **cross-pollination**: pollen generally has
  to travel *between* plants, not stay on one — which is why pollinators that move flower-to-flower
  are so valuable.

## Who does the moving: pollinators
Many plants rely on **animal pollinators** — bees (the most important for crops), butterflies,
moths, beetles, flies, birds, and bats — to carry pollen. Bees are especially effective because
they visit many flowers of the **same species** in one trip (flower constancy), which is exactly
the high-combo, sweep-a-patch behavior the game rewards.

## Why it matters: food
- Roughly **one-third (~35%) of global crop production volume** comes from plants that benefit from
  animal pollination. (FAO / Klein et al.)
- About **75% of the leading food crop types** depend on or are improved by pollinators.
- Pollinator-dependent crops include apples, almonds, blueberries, squash, coffee, and cocoa.
  Staples like wheat and rice are wind-pollinated, but the **variety, color, and nutrient density**
  of our diets leans heavily on pollinators.

## Threats to pollinators (mirrored as the game's hazards)
- **Pesticides** — insecticides (e.g. certain neonicotinoids) can poison or disorient bees. In the
  game, the **pesticide cloud** strips your pollen and stuns the bee — a direct stand-in.
- **Habitat loss & weather** — fewer flowers and harsher conditions stress colonies. The game's
  **wind gusts** and the relentless **wilt timer** stand in for environmental pressure.
- Also real but not modeled: disease/parasites (e.g. Varroa mite), and climate shifts that
  desync blooms and pollinators.

## What helps (the takeaway)
Plant diverse, native, flowering plants; provide continuous bloom; reduce pesticide use and avoid
spraying open flowers; leave nesting habitat. Healthy pollinators = more and better food.

## Talking points a player should leave with
1. Pollination = moving pollen so a plant can make **fruit and seed**.
2. It usually has to go **between flowers** — that's why moving pollinators matter.
3. **Bees** are the heavy lifters; **~1/3 of crops** depend on pollinators.
4. **Pesticides and habitat loss** threaten them — small choices help.
