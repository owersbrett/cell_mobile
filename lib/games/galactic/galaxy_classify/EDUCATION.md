# Galaxy Classifier — EDUCATION.md (the E: educational component)

## What the game teaches
The **Hubble morphological classification of galaxies** — the single most
important first cut astronomers make when they look at a galaxy. By the end of a
few rounds a player can look at any galaxy image and place it on the **Hubble
tuning fork**. This is real, durable knowledge that *compounds across
playthroughs*: the more galaxies you read, the faster and more accurate the next
round, because you are training pattern recognition on the same four shapes that
working astronomers use.

## The Hubble tuning fork (1926)
Edwin Hubble sorted galaxies into a fork-shaped diagram. The handle holds the
ellipticals; the fork splits into two prongs of spirals — unbarred on one prong,
barred on the other. Irregulars sit off the end. (Hubble called this a sequence
from "early" to "late" type — historical terms with **no** evolutionary meaning;
galaxies do not march down the fork over time.)

```
                          (unbarred spirals)
                         Sa --- Sb --- Sc
                        /
   E0 - E3 - E7 - S0 --<
   (ellipticals)  (lenticular)
                        \
                         SBa --- SBb --- SBc
                          (barred spirals)
```

## The four types you classify (and how to tell them apart)

### Spiral (S)
A flat, rotating disk with a central **bulge** and **spiral arms** of bright young
stars, gas, and dust winding out from the core. Read it by: a clear core **plus
arms**, no straight bar. Arm tightness varies — **Sa** are tightly wound with a
big bulge, **Sc** are loose and open with a small bulge. (In game: tighter winding
shows up at higher difficulty and is the subtle read.) The Milky Way and Andromeda
are spirals.

### Barred Spiral (SB)
Same disk-and-arms structure, but a straight **bar** of stars cuts across the
center, and **the arms trail off the ends of the bar** rather than the core. The
bar is a real dynamical feature — a standing wave that funnels gas inward. Roughly
**two-thirds of spiral galaxies are barred**, the Milky Way among them. Read it by:
spot the bar. That single feature is the whole distinction from a plain spiral —
which is exactly why it's the game's confusable pair.

### Elliptical (E)
A smooth, featureless ellipsoid of mostly **old, red stars**, with little gas,
dust, or ongoing star formation — and **no arms and no disk**. They range from
near-spherical (**E0**) to cigar-shaped (**E7**); the number is just how flattened
it looks. The biggest galaxies in the universe — giant ellipticals at the centers
of galaxy clusters — are this type, often built up by mergers. Read it by: a smooth
glowing blob that fades from a dense center, with **nothing** structured in it.

### Irregular (Irr)
No symmetry, no core, no arms — a **lumpy, chaotic** patchwork of star-forming
knots. Often small (dwarf irregulars) or galaxies distorted by a gravitational
interaction or merger. The Large and Small Magellanic Clouds, satellites of the
Milky Way, are the classic examples. Read it by: if it has no organizing shape at
all, it's irregular.

### (S0 — lenticular, the hinge)
Between ellipticals and spirals sit **lenticulars**: a disk and a bulge but **no
spiral arms** — a faded disk galaxy. The game keeps to the four primary buttons,
but S0 is named here because it's where the fork hinges, and it's a natural
"advanced type" to add later.

## Why morphology matters
Shape is a window into a galaxy's **history and contents**: spirals are
gas-rich, actively forming stars, and rotation-supported; ellipticals are
gas-poor, old, and supported by random stellar motions, frequently the end-state
of major mergers; irregulars are disturbed or primitive. Classifying a galaxy is
the first step toward asking *why* it looks that way. The same skill powers
citizen-science efforts like **Galaxy Zoo**, where hundreds of thousands of people
classify galaxy images by exactly these shapes to build catalogs no single team
could.

## The compounding loop (how the game rewards knowledge)
- Each round **reveals the true type** on every wrong call or miss — immediate,
  corrective feedback on the exact shape you misread.
- The **speed bonus** rewards recognizing a type *instantly*, which is precisely
  what improves with practice.
- The **difficulty ramp** introduces the hard cases (bars, tight spirals) only
  once the easy ones are second nature — so knowledge, not reflexes, is what
  carries a high score.
