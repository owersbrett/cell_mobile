# Molecule Mixer — How to Play

> Player-facing. Anyone reading this should understand the game. This is one entry in the
> Manual (and a source file for the deployable Manual app). Plain language, no code.

**Scale:** Molecular · **Type:** Solo (chase the high score) · **Length:** ~45 seconds

## The goal
A soup of labeled atom bubbles — H, O, C, N, Cl — drifts across the screen. At the top you
see a **target molecule** with its formula, its name, and the atoms you need. Tap the right
atoms to assemble it, one atom at a time, before the clock runs out.

## How to play
- Look at the **target panel** at the top: it shows the formula (e.g. H₂O) and a row of small
  circles for each atom you still need to place.
- Find the matching atom floating on screen and **tap it**. It flies into the construction zone
  in the center.
- If the molecule has a **core atom** (marked "CORE"), you must place that one first — the others
  won't snap in until it is. Tap the wrong atom first and an amber ring pulses to show you which
  one to place next. No penalty for this, just a nudge.
- **Tapping an atom you do not need** costs you 10 points and shakes it red.
- When all slots are filled, the molecule completes: you earn **+30 points and +4 seconds** on
  the clock. Then the next target appears immediately.
- The longer the round goes, the faster the atoms move and the more complex the targets get.

## How you score
- **+5** for each correct atom placed.
- **+30 + 4 seconds** when a molecule is fully assembled.
- **−10** for tapping an atom the current target does not need.

## Did you know? (the fun part)
Every time you complete a molecule, a card appears for a moment telling you **where that molecule
lives in a real potato** — or what it does for one. Completing CO₂ tells you it is the carbon
source that potato leaves absorb to build starch. Completing O₂ tells you potato mitochondria
burn it to make energy. None of this changes your score; it is just there to discover.

## Tips
- Prioritize the **core atom first** — in H₂O that is O, in CO₂ and CH₄ that is C.
- The field is always stocked with what you need. If you do not see a needed atom, scan the
  edges — they drift in from there.
- Comboing molecules back-to-back (each one adding 4 seconds) is the path to high scores.

## What it teaches
How atoms bond into real molecules — water, oxygen, carbon dioxide, methane, ammonia — and
what those molecules mean for the plant that becomes your food.
