# EDUCATION.md — pH Balance (molecular scale)

> This game's educational slice. Scale-wide context: `../EDUCATION.md`.
> **The learning arc:** atoms → elements bond into molecules → those molecules ARE the
> potato. pH Balance teaches the rule that governs how those molecules behave in water:
> **acidity, the 0–14 pH scale, and neutralization.**

- **Scale (cell):** molecular
- **Concept taught:** acids vs bases · the pH scale · indicators · neutralization · the
  titration curve and its steep equivalence point.

---

## What the mechanic teaches (education IN the verb)

The teaching is not a popup — it is the controls themselves.

1. **The 0–14 pH scale is a real object.** The right-edge strip is a universal-indicator
   ramp: red (strong acid, 0) → orange → yellow → **green (neutral, 7)** → blue → indigo →
   **violet (strong base, 14)**. The beaker liquid is the same color as its current pH. A
   player who plays a round can read pH off a color for the rest of their life.

2. **Acids lower pH, bases raise it — and they cancel.** The ACID button is literally
   labeled **H⁺ (pH ▼)** and the BASE button **OH⁻ (pH ▲)**. Adding acid to a basic liquid
   walks it down toward 7; adding base to an acidic one walks it up. The player *feels*
   neutralization: opposing reagents driving toward the middle.

3. **The titration curve is steep near neutral.** This is the headline lesson and the whole
   difficulty of the game. Each drop's effect is multiplied by a gaussian peaked at pH 7, so
   near neutral one drop **leaps** the needle across the equivalence point and overshoots —
   exactly like the near-vertical jump in a real acid–base titration curve. Far from 7 the
   liquid is sluggish (buffered). The player learns *where to be gentle*: tiny inputs near 7,
   bigger ones at the extremes.

4. **CO₂ acidifies water (carbonic acid).** At higher levels the liquid slowly self-acidifies
   — the same chemistry by which dissolved CO₂ forms carbonic acid and lowers the pH of rain,
   oceans, and soil. The player must keep adding base to hold a basic target: homeostasis as
   a verb.

## Potato angle

Potatoes prefer slightly acidic soil (~pH 5.0–6.0). Growers **titrate their fields**: lime
(a base) to raise pH, elemental sulfur (acidifying) to lower it. Common scab is suppressed in
acidic soil and worsens when fields are over-limed toward neutral — so a grower deliberately
*holds the band* below 7, which is exactly the game's near-neutral danger zone reframed.
Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is itself a weak acid; cut-potato browning is pH-sensitive (acidulated
water slows it). The beaker is the field and the storage bin; balancing pH is balancing the
potato's chemistry. The titration in your hands is potato agronomy at bench scale.

## Association verdict

**KEEP — strong, unique tie.** pH Balance is the only game in the catalog that teaches the
acid–base / pH / neutralization concept, and it teaches it through the controls (H⁺ vs OH⁻),
the indicator color, and the steep-near-7 difficulty — not through text. The potato bridge
(soil pH, liming, scab suppression) is real agronomy, not metaphor.
