# Molecule Builder — How to Play

> Player-facing. Anyone reading this should understand the game. This is one entry in the
> Manual (and a source file for the deployable Manual app). Plain language, no code.

**Scale:** Molecular · **Type:** Solo (score and survive) · **Length:** 90 seconds

## The goal
Atoms drift around the screen on their own. Order cards at the top show molecules you need to
build — each with a countdown bar ticking down. Drag atoms together to form the right molecule
before the order expires. Run out of lives (by letting orders expire) and the round ends early.

## How to play
- **Drag any atom** around the field. As you drag it near others, dashed lines appear showing
  possible bonds.
- **Release** to try the combination. If the atoms nearby add up to a valid recipe, they snap
  together, disappear, and score.
- The combination must **exactly match one of the order cards** at the top for full points.
  If it matches a real molecule but nothing in the order queue, you still get a small bonus.
- If the atoms nearby do not form any recipe, they **scatter** (repulsion burst) and a red flash
  appears — no penalty, but you have wasted a moment.
- Atoms also **form on their own** if they drift close enough — chemistry does not wait for you.
  This can accidentally fill an order (free points) or consume atoms you needed (bad luck).
- When an order's countdown bar reaches zero: you lose a life and the card flashes red. You start
  with **5 lives**.
- Score enough quickly enough, and the orders get harder (more atoms, more complex molecules).

## How you score
- Filling an order scores that order's **base points**, boosted by a **combo multiplier** if you
  filled the previous order within 3 seconds.
- A bonus build (valid molecule, no order match) scores a flat **+5**.
- Losing a life costs nothing in points — but zero lives ends the round.

## Tips
- Focus on the **shortest-countdown order first** — the order with the least time remaining is
  the one that will cost you a life next.
- Simple molecules (H₂O, CO₂) are fast to fill early. Later orders need 5–8 atoms; cluster
  multiple atoms before you release.
- The magnetic pull toward the dragged atom helps you cluster — you do not need to place atoms
  directly on top of each other, just close enough.

## What it teaches
How specific atoms combine into real molecules — water, carbon dioxide, ammonia, methane — and
that the combinations are not arbitrary: the ratio of each atom type is chemistry, not game design.
